The success button: green and lime today, and the change we are making

AutoVex has three button components, two of them deprecated, and the act of accepting is currently drawn in three different colours depending on which side of the marketplace you are on and which component the surface happens to use. This page shows exactly what is on screen today, then the change we would like to make, with every state rendered and every contrast ratio measured from the rendered pixels.

Audience: devs and designers, both sides of the product. Read against the 2026-09-16 production dump. Not part of the prototype.
Status: decided and announced to the dealership product team, 2026-09-17. Sections 1–3 describe production as it stands today; section 4 onwards is what was agreed.

In short

  • The seller's two accept buttons are two different colours — blue on the offer card, green on the modal footer — because they are two different components.
  • The dealership's three accept buttons are one colour, lime, from the shared intent="success".
  • intent="success" already means green-600 in the icon components. The same intent name resolves to two hues in one design system.
  • Lime fails WCAG AA in 7 of its 14 states, including three that are not transient. The cause is the label, not the hue — see the next point.
  • Contrast decides the label, not the colour. Once the primary label is black, both hues pass, and lime actually scores higher than green. So the accessibility case settles lime-900 and white out of the running; it does not, on its own, choose between lime and green.
  • What was decided: the seller side migrates its accepts onto AButton, the current component, and AButton's success intent goes from lime to green-400 with a black label, all five variants. No new component, no new intent. The argument for green over corrected lime is the icon inconsistency above, plus the seller-side view that lime does not read as an accept.
  • The dealership side is not touched, and that is deliberate. Their accepts run on UiButton, which is also deprecated and which this change leaves alone. Their buttons turn green when they migrate to AButton on a ticket of their own — see section 4.

1 — Three button components, two of them deprecated

This is the part that is easy to get wrong: there is not one deprecated button and one current one. There are two deprecated layers.

ComponentStatusConsumersColour API
js/components/elements/Button.vue deprecated 17 a color prop taking 'blue' | 'red' | 'green-600' | 'transparent' — a palette step baked into the API
js/components/ui/UiButton.vue deprecated 78 intent + variant, semantic. success = lime
app/components/atoms/AButton/AButton.vue current 7 intent + variant, semantic. success = lime, byte-for-byte the same values as UiButton

Both deprecated components carry the same JSDoc: use the TypeScript atom instead. UiButton has 78 consumers against AButton's 7, so the migration it asks for has barely started — which matters below, because the lime success definition is currently duplicated in both files and a colour change has to land in both until one of them is gone.

The inconsistency worth naming first

intent="success" is not only a button prop. UiIcon.vue and ASpriteIcon.vue both map it to text-green-600.

So the design system already says success is green — in every component except the one where it is most visible. A dealership accept today puts a lime button next to green success icons. Changing the button is not introducing a new meaning for green; it is making the button agree with the icon.

4 — The change, as agreed

One change, arriving on each side separately

Read this part carefully — it is the thing most easily got wrong. AButton is the current button component. Neither side uses it for accepts today. The seller side is on elements/Button.vue and ui/UiButton.vue; the dealership is on ui/UiButton.vue. Both of those are deprecated. So the two sides are one generation apart, not one of them already on target.

Seller side — now
Component change and colour change.

Both accepts come off the two deprecated components and onto AButton intent="success". The card stops being blue, the modal loses its hardcoded color="green-600", and both render the new green.

Dealership side — nothing now
Unchanged until they migrate. Then both at once.

Their three accepts stay lime. They run on UiButton, which this change does not touch. When they migrate to AButton on a ticket of their own, the buttons turn green as part of that move — so it is not a no-op refactor and should not be scoped as one.

Where this lands: one button component and one success colour across both sides — reached in two steps, one per team, instead of one deploy that changes another team's UI for them.

  1. Take the seller side off the deprecated components. The modal accept moves from Button color="green-600" to AButton intent="success"; the card accept moves from a bare variant="primary" to the same. Both seller accepts then match each other.
  2. Redefine success from lime to green in AButton, and only there. UiButton carries its own copy of the same values and is deliberately left alone.
  3. Move all five variants, not only primary. default, primary, secondary, ghost and link each have their own compound row. Leaving any of them lime would put two greens in one intent.

Why AButton only, rather than editing both files

The success values are duplicated byte-for-byte in UiButton and AButton. Editing both would turn the dealership's buttons green on our deploy, without their team doing anything or choosing when. Editing only AButton was the call, for three reasons:

  • AButton has zero success consumers today, so the colour edit ships with no visual change anywhere. The change arrives with each migration, one at a time, reviewable on its own.
  • No user sees both sides. A seller never opens the dealership UI, so the interim lime-here-green-there split is a design-system inconsistency, not a user-facing one. That is what makes waiting cost nothing.
  • It gives the dealership team an easy path rather than an obligation: nothing to do now, and the colour comes free with a migration they want anyway.

For whoever writes the dealership's migration ticket

It is not a no-op refactor. Scoped as "swap the component, should look identical", the colour change reads as a regression and gets reverted. Scope it as a visual change. It is small otherwise:

  • 3 files, ~6 button instances — DealerAcceptCounterOfferAction, DeliveryAgreedActions, PastDeliveryDateConfirmModal
  • the icon props carry over unchanged — check-bold and flag-checkered-fill are both in the shared sprite directory that feeds either component

And the migration is worth doing on its own merits. UiButton is missing three things AButton has: the accent (turquoise) intent, the nav-cta variant, and px-0 on iconOnly — which AButton's own source names as a defect in UiButton, "leaving px-8 fighting w-14 at lg". The deprecated file has already drifted, which is what a duplicated cva does.

Why the label goes black rather than staying on the green ramp

Lime pairs its primary fill with lime-900. Mirroring that with green-900 works at rest and breaks on hover, which is the same trap lime is already in. Black is what lets the fill stay bright enough to read as the primary action while every state passes.

Fillblackgreen-900green-950white
green-400 (rest)
green-500 (hover)
green-600 (active)

green-950 is the one tinted label that survives the whole ladder, and it is a legitimate alternative to black if a fully neutral label reads as too hard. It is within a hair of black visually at that darkness.

5 — All five variants, three ways

This is the part that has to be agreed rather than assumed. Changing success touches five compound rows, and each one has three states. Every tile below is the resolved class string rendered statically so it can be measured; the label size is the md size, 14px, so the large-text allowance does not apply and the bar is 4.5:1 throughout.

Neither hue fixes itself by changing hue. green-700 on the green-200/300/400 fills fails exactly as lime-700 does on its own ramp. Both sets therefore need the same three corrections, each forced by a number rather than chosen:

  • primary label: black, not the ramp's own -900
  • secondary label: the -900 step, not -700
  • default / ghost / link active: keep the -700 rather than lightening to -600

Why the middle set is here

Asked by the dealership-side designer, and it is the right question: the proposal changes two things at once — the hue and the label — so which one is doing the work? The middle set answers it. Apply the same three corrections to lime and it passes everywhere too, scoring higher than green at rest and hover, because lime-400 is a lighter fill than green-400 and black has more room on it. Contrast chooses the label. It does not choose the hue.

Honest framing for the conversation

Lime's active failures are the weakest part of the case — an active state is held only while the pointer is down, and how strictly WCAG applies to it is arguable. The three that are not arguable are primary hover, and secondary at rest and on hover. Those are steady states a user reads.

And this is not a lime-only problem: the default (blue) intent fails in three of its own states too — default hover 4.13, default active 2.67, secondary active 3.41. The honest version of this ask is success is the worst of them and it is the one we are opening anyway, not lime is uniquely broken. A pass across every intent is its own piece of work.

And the strongest correction of all: with the label fixed, lime passes too. Do not take an accessibility argument into this conversation as though it settles the hue — it settles lime-900 and white, both of which lose on either ramp. What remains for green is that the icon components already call success green, and a seller-side view that lime does not read as an accept. Those are the arguments worth making.

7 — What this does not touch

Lime does not leave the product. It stays wherever it is a status colour rather than an action colour, and none of these read intent from a button.

  • UiBadge's lime and light_lime variants — including the seller's own auction-ended badge
  • UiTab's success tab
  • OProductCard and OContainer success framing
  • the B2B reserve-price surfaces — ReservePriceNotice, B2BReserveStatusBadge

Worth deciding separately whether those should follow. The argument for leaving them is that a lime status and a green action is a usable distinction; the argument against is that we would then have two success colours on purpose. Not part of this proposal either way.

8 — What the dealership side needs to do

Announced 2026-09-17. Short answer: nothing now.

  • Nothing changes for you on this deploy. Your accepts stay lime; UiButton is untouched.
  • Acknowledgement, and a heads-up when a related ticket comes up so the timing can be lined up.
  • When you do migrate, use the ticket card above — three files, and it carries a visual change rather than being a pure refactor.
  • Still open, and yours to answer whenever you get there: black vs green-950 for the primary label, and whether green-400 is the right step. The comparison that picked it was scoped to the seller's accept. If there is a reason to prefer a different green, the rest of the case is unaffected — it is about one token and one component, not this exact step.
