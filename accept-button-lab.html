The success button: green and lime today, and the change we propose

AutoVex has three button components, two of them deprecated, and the act of accepting is currently drawn in three different colours depending on which side of the marketplace you are on and which component the surface happens to use. This page shows exactly what is on screen today, then the change we would like to make, with every state rendered and every contrast ratio measured from the rendered pixels.

Audience: devs and designers, both sides of the product. Read against the 2026-09-16 production dump. Not part of the prototype.

In short

  • The seller's two accept buttons are two different colours — blue on the offer card, green on the modal footer — because they are two different components.
  • The dealership's three accept buttons are one colour, lime, from the shared intent="success".
  • intent="success" already means green-600 in the icon components. The same intent name resolves to two hues in one design system.
  • Lime fails WCAG AA in 7 of its 14 states, including three that are not transient.
  • The proposal: retire the deprecated components on the seller side, put every accept on the shared intent="success", and redefine that intent from lime to green. No new component, no new intent, one token for both sides.

1 — Three button components, two of them deprecated

This is the part that is easy to get wrong: there is not one deprecated button and one current one. There are two deprecated layers.

ComponentStatusConsumersColour API
js/components/elements/Button.vue deprecated 17 a color prop taking 'blue' | 'red' | 'green-600' | 'transparent' — a palette step baked into the API
js/components/ui/UiButton.vue deprecated 78 intent + variant, semantic. success = lime
app/components/atoms/AButton/AButton.vue current 7 intent + variant, semantic. success = lime, byte-for-byte the same values as UiButton

Both deprecated components carry the same JSDoc: use the TypeScript atom instead. UiButton has 78 consumers against AButton's 7, so the migration it asks for has barely started — which matters below, because the lime success definition is currently duplicated in both files and a colour change has to land in both until one of them is gone.

The inconsistency worth naming first

intent="success" is not only a button prop. UiIcon.vue and ASpriteIcon.vue both map it to text-green-600.

So the design system already says success is green — in every component except the one where it is most visible. A dealership accept today puts a lime button next to green success icons. Changing the button is not introducing a new meaning for green; it is making the button agree with the icon.

4 — The change we propose

Three steps, and they are separable — step 1 can ship without step 2.

  1. Take the seller side off the deprecated components. The modal accept moves from Button color="green-600" to intent="success"; the card accept moves from a bare variant="primary" to the same intent. Both seller accepts then match each other and match the dealership.
  2. Redefine success from lime to green in the cva. One edit per file, and every consumer moves together.
  3. Move all five variants, not only primary. default, primary, secondary, ghost and link each have their own compound row. Leaving any of them lime would put two greens in one intent.

Why the label goes black rather than staying on the green ramp

Lime pairs its primary fill with lime-900. Mirroring that with green-900 works at rest and breaks on hover, which is the same trap lime is already in. Black is what lets the fill stay bright enough to read as the primary action while every state passes.

Fillblackgreen-900green-950white
green-400 (rest)
green-500 (hover)
green-600 (active)

green-950 is the one tinted label that survives the whole ladder, and it is a legitimate alternative to black if a fully neutral label reads as too hard. It is within a hair of black visually at that darkness.

5 — All five variants, lime against green

This is the part that has to be agreed rather than assumed. Changing success touches five compound rows, and each one has three states. Every tile below is the resolved class string rendered statically so it can be measured; the label size is the md size, 14px, so the large-text allowance does not apply and the bar is 4.5:1 throughout.

A straight lime → green swap does not fix this on its own. green-700 on the green-200/300/400 fills fails exactly as lime-700 does. The proposal therefore deviates from the mirror in three places, each forced by a number rather than chosen:

  • primary label: black, not green-900
  • secondary label: green-900, not green-700
  • default / ghost / link active: keep green-700 rather than lightening to green-600

Honest framing for the conversation

Lime's active failures are the weakest part of the case — an active state is held only while the pointer is down, and how strictly WCAG applies to it is arguable. The three that are not arguable are primary hover, and secondary at rest and on hover. Those are steady states a user reads.

And this is not a lime-only problem: the default (blue) intent fails in three of its own states too — default hover 4.13, default active 2.67, secondary active 3.41. The honest version of this ask is success is the worst of them and it is the one we are opening anyway, not lime is uniquely broken. A pass across every intent is its own piece of work.

7 — What this does not touch

Lime does not leave the product. It stays wherever it is a status colour rather than an action colour, and none of these read intent from a button.

  • UiBadge's lime and light_lime variants — including the seller's own auction-ended badge
  • UiTab's success tab
  • OProductCard and OContainer success framing
  • the B2B reserve-price surfaces — ReservePriceNotice, B2BReserveStatusBadge

Worth deciding separately whether those should follow. The argument for leaving them is that a lime status and a green action is a usable distinction; the argument against is that we would then have two success colours on purpose. Not part of this proposal either way.

8 — What we need from the dealership side

  • Agreement that success should be green rather than lime, given that the icon components already say so.
  • A view on black vs green-950 for the primary label.
  • A check on the three surfaces this changes on that side — accept a counter offer, confirm the sale, mark the handover completed. All three are confirmations, so the meaning does not move; only the hue does.
  • Whether green-400 is the right step. It won the seller-side comparison on contrast, but that comparison was scoped to the seller's accept. If the dealership side has a reason to prefer a different green, the case here is unaffected — it is about one token and about lime failing, not about this exact step.
