Design initiative · contact step

AutoVex exclusive

Would sellers hold off selling or listing their car elsewhere until the AutoVex auction ends, in exchange for free extra visibility? A painted-door test: a required yes-or-no question on the contact step, and a thank-you on the decision page for those who said yes. Nothing is built behind it yet.

Status: variant 2 chosen · copy is a draft October 2026

Summary

What to build

  • Contact step (section 1): every seller answers a required question, “Haluatko autollesi ilmaista lisänäkyvyyttä?” (do you want free extra visibility for your car?). Yes means not selling the car, through any channel, or listing it for sale elsewhere, from submitting the ad until the AutoVex auction ends, about 5 business days. Asking dealerships for offers is not ruled out.
  • Decision page (section 2): sellers who said yes are thanked for waiting, inside the warm-up card's and the hero's own copy. No reminder, no new element.
  • A painted door. The answer is recorded and nothing checks it. The extra visibility is not built on the buyer side in this test.
  • The test is control against this variant.

Contact step · warm-up · decision page

What it is meant to show

How many sellers would wait for AutoVex's offers, and whether those who say yes are less likely to reject the offers because they already sold the car. The question is required, so the yes rate is a share of every seller who submits.

During the test

Customer service contacts about the commitment are followed, and the copy iterated on them. The effect of the stated period on the yes rate is tested in a follow-up A/B test.

1 — Contact step: required visibility question

Chosen · draft copyContact stepEvery seller, new ad or edit

What changes

  • A required yes-or-no question, between the email field and the consent checkboxes, with the funnel's group-legend heading and a red asterisk: “Haluatko autollesi ilmaista lisänäkyvyyttä?” (do you want free extra visibility for your car?). The value, free included, comes before any commitment is asked.
  • Two option cards, the funnel's own radio cards: yes, on the condition first with the green “recommended” tag at the right end of the label's first line, inside the card (the label wraps beside it), no second. Each has a short description, and no explanation sits below them. Neither is preselected.
  • The two answers mirror each other. “Kyllä, haluan ilmaista lisänäkyvyyttä” (yes, I want free extra visibility) and “En halua ilmaista lisänäkyvyyttä” (I don't want free extra visibility) answer the question in its own words, so the choice reads as yes or no to the same offer. Neither asks the seller to commit to a sale.
  • The yes description states the condition as the exchange. “Vastineeksi sitoudun siihen, etten myy autoani enkä ilmoita sitä myyntiin muualla ennen AutoVexin tarjouskilpailun päättymistä (noin 5 arkipäivää)” (in return, I commit to not selling my car or listing it for sale elsewhere before the AutoVex auction ends, about 5 business days). “Vastineeksi” (in return) ties it to the visibility the label asks for, and the closing “Lisänäkyvyys voi nostaa tarjouksia” (extra visibility can raise the offers) says what it may lead to, hedged with “voi” (can). It rules out an earlier sale, including a private sale or a trade-in, which is what turns into an “I already sold the car” rejection, and listing the car elsewhere in the meantime. The period runs from submitting the ad until the auction ends. Asking dealerships for offers is deliberately not ruled out.
  • The standard option does not mention selling elsewhere, so it cannot read as advice to list the car on other sites.
  • Submitting without a choice is blocked with the funnel's own “selection required” error on the options.
  • Shown to every seller, logged in or not, and the earlier choice is restored when they come back to edit.
  • Shown nowhere else in the product.

Acceptance criteria

  • Variant sellers see the question above the consent checkboxes, logged in or not, with neither option selected on first visit.
  • Submitting without a choice shows “Valinta vaaditaan.” on the options and scrolls to them; picking one clears it.
  • Returning to the contact step shows the seller's last choice, and it can be changed.
  • The choice is recorded per ad with the arm.
  • Nothing else in the product changes based on the choice.

2 — Decision page: thanks on the warm-up and the hero

Chosen · draft copyDecision pageSeller answered yes, decision open

What changes

  • Who sees it: only sellers who answered yes on the contact step, and only while the page shows the offers-waiting card and hero. No new element: the thanks is part of the existing copy.
  • Warm-up card: its heading and body change; image, badge and button stay. Seen once, before the offers are revealed.
  • Decision page hero: the heading stays (“Nyt on aikasi toimia!”) and a sentence leads its body. It stays for as long as the hero does: once a negotiation, a final offer or a closed negotiation replaces the hero with its own message, the thanks goes with it.
  • The tone assumes the best. The copy thanks the seller for waiting until the auction ended, which is what they agreed to. It never reminds them of a promise or asks them to keep it: no “commitment” or “promise” words at this point. A thank-you does the reminding without any negative feeling towards AutoVex at the moment the seller decides.

Decision page hero, body (heading unchanged)

Your listing got extra visibility because you waited until the auction ended. Accept the offer and the dealership will contact you.

Unchanged text is production's. The warm-up and the hero say the same two things in a different order, so a seller who sees both on their first visit does not read the same sentence twice. Neither mentions the earlier choice (“you chose”): a seller will not remember a question from days ago, and the thanks carries the condition.

Acceptance criteria

  • The warm-up heading and body change only for yes-sellers with offers waiting; every other seller sees today's card.
  • The hero's body leads with the thanks only for yes-sellers, and only while the hero is the offers-waiting one; any other hero is unchanged.
  • Rejections are recorded with the seller's answer, so the “I already sold the car” rate can be compared between yes and no.

Why: the commitment exists to stop the car being sold before the seller decides on the offers; thanking at the decision moment closes that loop without pressure.

What to read from it

  • Yes rate: sellers answering yes, out of all who submitted the contact step in the variant.
  • Did the question cost anything? Contact-step completion, variant against control. A required question before the submit button could make some sellers hesitate or drop off.
  • “I already sold the car” rejections: their share among yes-sellers, no-sellers and control. This is the outcome the commitment is meant to move.
  • Who says yes: split by review segment and by the seller-intent answer where one exists, to see whether committed sellers are also the ones ready to sell.

Open questions

  • The visibility is promised and not delivered. The contact step offers it, and the decision page says “Ilmoituksesi sai lisänäkyvyyttä” (your listing got extra visibility), which is not true during the test. Do we accept that for the test's length, tell sellers afterwards, or keep only the thanks on the decision page?
  • Two durations in one funnel. The funnel and emails tell sellers an auction typically lasts 36 hours; this question says about 5 business days until the auction ends. Both are right for what they describe, but a seller may compare them. (The 36 hours is also shorter than the auction's real schedule of three to four business days, shortened by the first offer.)
  • The period starts at submit. A seller who already has the car listed elsewhere would have to take that listing down to answer yes truthfully. Intended, but it may lower the yes rate.
  • Both decision-page touches, or one? The warm-up and the hero both carry the thanks; whether to keep both is decided when the test is scoped.
  • Option order. The yes option is first, and the first option tends to be picked more. Randomising the order per seller would remove that bias.
  • Legal. The wording asks a consumer to commit to something, even without enforcement. Does it need a legal read before going live?
