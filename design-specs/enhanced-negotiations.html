Enhanced negotiations

Almost half of all offers go to a negotiation, and the usual reason one fails is the seller asking too much. Every fact that would tell them what is reasonable already exists — and none of it is on screen when they type a number.

Ready to build — Finnish copy pending approval August 2026

Goal

  • More educated counter offers — the seller can see what they are countering.
  • Fewer counter offers — not discouraged, just better judged.
  • More acceptance, either side — the tertial goal this belongs to.

Not a goal: more sellers countering. Half of all offers already reach negotiation, which is a heavy load for dealerships. Each change is judged on the quality of an ask, not the likelihood of one.

Negotiation logic does not change. Two rounds each, the amount caps and what an acceptance does all stay exactly as they are. Everything below is what the seller sees.

How to read this

Each numbered change is one self-contained piece of work — a candidate ticket. Every section states what changes, where it is, which state the seller has to be in to see it, and links straight to that state in the prototype. Finnish copy carries an English gloss for reference; the reasoning is in small print at the end and is context, not requirement.

Three surfaces are involved: the negotiation modal, the offer card and hero on the decision page, and three emails. Nothing else is touched.

Order matters in one place: change 1 creates the block that changes 2, 3 and 9 put copy into, and changes 4 and 5 are the same component. Everything else is independent.

1 — Guidance in a help block

Negotiation modalAll states

What changes

  • A new contained block holds the modal’s guidance: peach background #FAEADA, 1px border of the same colour, 6px radius, 16px padding (12px below 620px), 14px slate-600 text.
  • It sits directly under the header, above the thread and the form.
  • One item renders as a paragraph, several as a bulleted list.
  • Used in all three states — before sending, waiting for a reply, and closed. Build this first: changes 2, 3 and 9 all put their copy inside it.

The block, before sending

Autoliike voi hyväksyä vastatarjouksesi, jolloin kaupat syntyvät. Se voi myös nostaa tai pitäytyä tarjouksessaan.

See it in the prototype — open Tee vastatarjous on the highest offer

Why: Guidance, heading and form currently run together as one column of body copy at the same size, with nothing marking where the explanation ends and the seller’s task begins. The delivery-date modal in the product already uses this exact block for the same purpose.

2 — Counter offer outcomes

Negotiation modalBefore sending

What changes

  • The help block states what can happen to a counter offer, in one sentence pair:
  • “Autoliike voi hyväksyä vastatarjouksesi, jolloin kaupat syntyvät. Se voi myös nostaa tai pitäytyä tarjouksessaan.”
    EN: The dealership can accept your counter offer, which closes the sale. It can also raise its offer or stand by it.
  • Both current bullets are removed: “Tee vastatarjous eniten tarjonneelle” and “Vastatarjouksen avulla päästään usein kauppoihin”.

See it in the prototype — open Tee vastatarjous on the highest offer

Why: The first restates the action the seller has already taken; the second encourages more counter offers, which is explicitly not this initiative’s goal. What is left are the two facts that decide whether an ask is reasonable: an accepted counter offer closes the sale on the spot, and the dealership’s offer can never fall.

4 — Negotiation thread redesign

Negotiation modalAny negotiation

What changes

  • Reuse the negotiation thread component from the buyer (dealership) view. It already supports a seller point of view; the seller side is simply still on the old one. This is a swap, not a new component.
  • Messages become bordered cards on a light background instead of solid blue fills with a tail: the seller’s own right-aligned with a blue border and pale blue fill, the dealership’s left-aligned with a grey border on white, each with the corner nearest the sender squared off.

  • The accept button sits inside the dealership’s last message, as it does on the buyer side.

5 — Auction result as a bubble

Negotiation modalAll states

What changes

  • The winning auction offer renders as the first message in the thread, on the dealership’s side, labelled Korkein tarjous (EN: Highest offer) with the amount in bold and the auction end time as its timestamp.
  • It is present even when no negotiation exists yet, so the modal always opens with a thread and the offer being countered is always on screen while the seller types.
  • It never changes as the negotiation continues, and never carries the accept button.

See it in the prototype — open Tee vastatarjous on the highest offer

Why: On a first counter offer the modal shows no figure at all today — and it covers the offer the seller was just reading, so they close it to check and reopen. As a bubble the negotiation reads in order: what was offered, what was asked, what came back. A figure quoted beside the form was tried twice, as a line and as a grey hint badge, and both read as a widget bolted onto the field.

10 — Auto-close message

Negotiation threadAutomatically closed

What changes

  • When a negotiation closes automatically because nobody answered in time, the closing message reads “Neuvottelu on nyt päättynyt. Viimeisin tarjous odottaa päätöstäsi.”
    EN: The negotiation has now ended. The latest offer is waiting for your decision.
  • It currently reuses the dealership’s own pre-filled closing message, which begins “Hei ja kiitos viestistäsi…” — Hello and thank you for your message…

Why: An unanswered negotiation currently ends with a message written as though the dealership had replied. The honest string already exists and is used by a sibling process; the seller cannot otherwise tell a dealership’s decision from a deadline.

15 — Updated green

Decision page · card and modalAll states

What changes

  • Accept is green-400 (#4ADE80) with a black label, hover green-500 (#22C55E) — darker on hover, like every other button in the product. The label stays black; the hover is 9.22:1.
  • Same value in four places: the card, the modal footer, the in-message accept, and the confirmation button on the accept confirmation step — the one shown after the seller presses the green button.
  • The in-message accept carries the card’s own label — Hyväksy korkein tarjous — with no amount and no icon. The amount is the bold figure at the top of the same bubble.

This one is decided by contrast, not taste. The label is 14px medium, so it needs 4.5:1 — the 3:1 large-text allowance does not apply. White text cannot reach that on any green bright enough to read as the primary action: today’s accept green is 3.30:1 against white, and green-500 is 2.28:1. Moving the contrast into the label instead of darkening the fill keeps the button bright and lands at 12.05:1, comfortably AAA. Every candidate was measured on the contrast lab page; a text shadow, border or glow would not have changed any of those numbers.

See it in the prototype — the dealership has replied — open Näytä vastaus, then press the green accept to see the confirmation step

Why: The current accept green is a slightly darker shade; 500 is the brand green and sits better beside the card’s blues. Repeating the amount on the button made the two accepts read as different actions.

Needs a decision before build

Should the seller be told the dealership’s response deadline?

A dealership has 24 hours of business time to answer a counter offer. Miss it and the negotiation closes automatically at noon the next working day, with the standing offer left intact — that is the mechanic change 10 rests on.

Today the consumer UI never states it. The waiting state says only that replies usually come the same day; the 24 hours a seller does see in the round-1 and round-2 emails is how long their window to accept lasts, not how long the dealership has. The deadline is currently communicated to dealerships only.

An earlier draft of this spec put it in the modal’s waiting state. It was replaced by the line that now sits there — “Voit hyväksyä tarjouksen myös neuvottelujen aikana.” — which answers a different question. Both do not fit without the block growing again.

The call: state the deadline and accept a taller waiting state, keep the current line and leave the deadline unstated, or find a shorter form that carries both. Nothing is built either way until this is decided.

Out of scope

Idea Why not
"Most deals close within X% of the highest offer"A nudge towards a number. It would have us setting the price rather than explaining the service.
A suggested or prefilled counter offerSame, more strongly. Facts by the field; the seller names the price.
Warning that the dealership may close the negotiationReads as a threat, and presents a mitigation for over-asking as a rule of the game. Change 2 does the same work from the reassuring end.
The "four or more offers" signalA car with four or more offers has reached market price. That belongs to steering the seller towards accepting and has its own initiatives; inside the negotiation modal it would steer them away from an action they have already chosen.
Showing the seller's own estimate or asking priceNever echoed back today, and both fields may be sunset.
A seller confirmation when the dealership acceptsChanges negotiation logic and adds a way for a deal to stall.
The two-round structure and the amount capsThis initiative explains the mechanics; it does not alter them.

Design goals

What this initiative is trying to move, and how we would see it.

Goal Signal
More offers accepted, either sideAcceptance rate up. The outcome this belongs to.
Counter offers land closer to what is achievableThe counter offer against the standing offer — closer, less spread. The best signal the facts landed.
Fewer negotiations killed by an unreachable askDealerships closing before the seller's second round — down.
Fewer counter offers, better judgedVolume down, and that is fine. Never read on its own.
Sellers stop asking us how a negotiation worksNegotiation support contacts down. Needs change 9's decision-page route left intact.

User testing should show it before the numbers do. Several of these changes are plain usability fixes rather than persuasion — a thread that opens on its newest message, an error that names the right field, a status the seller can find without hunting. The next round of testing should show fewer of the struggles already recorded around the negotiation: leaving the modal to check the highest offer, not knowing whether a counter offer is binding, and not finding the dealership's reply after following an email.

No negotiation analytics exist today, so "sellers ask too much" is a known cause of unknown size. The counter-offer-against-standing-offer distribution is the number that would tell us whether facts alone can move it.
