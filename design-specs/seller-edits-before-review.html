Seller edits before review

Once a seller submits their ad and it waits for a review call, they cannot change anything about their car. This initiative lets them edit it while it waits, before an advisor picks it up.

Status: ideation · 2 changes in the prototype · nothing agreed September 2026

How to read this

Nothing here is agreed yet. This page holds the direction so far, the first change as a prototype variant, and the questions still open.

The prototype's default state has been matched to production for every edit flow first, so a future variant is compared against what sellers really get today.

What we are solving

  • Fewer ads rejected just so the seller can fix them. Today the only way to let a seller change an ad waiting for review is for an advisor to reject it for ad quality, most often to get service book photos added. That is manual work spent on something the seller could do themselves.
  • Fewer sellers lost after the review call. Many sellers who are sent back after the call never return to finish.
  • Editing only after a rejection, or only during the auction, is the wrong moment. The seller should be able to fix the ad while it waits.
  • Advisors keep what they do today. They can still book ads whose seller has not verified their email, and have the seller verify it, or verify it for them, during the call.

Editing a rejected ad from the offers page takes two clicks today, and depends on timing. Both of its edit buttons first open the waiting screen (funnel success) showing the rejection; the seller then presses “Päivitä tarjouspyyntöä” to reach the funnel steps.

That happens because the funnel resumes the position it last saved, and that saved position expires two weeks after the ad entered review. After that, the same buttons land straight on the equipment step. The prototype reproduces the two-click path, since that is what production does. A new edit entry built on the same mechanism would inherit both behaviours.

Direction so far

Decided

Editing before the ad is published goes through the funnel. The auction edit modal saves to the published ad, and an ad waiting for review is not published yet, so the funnel is the editor that already owns this data.

Any link back to the funnel is enough to begin with. A link that opens a specific step would save clicks and avoid the timing issue above; it may join the initiative later.

Leaning towards

No new “ad updated” screen. After editing, the seller returns to the view they started from and is greeted with a notification in a design that already exists: the success toast production shows when an auction edit is saved. Its wording here is “Tarjouspyyntö päivitetty” (Listing updated); the auction edit keeps “Tarjouspyyntö tallennettu” (Listing saved). It keeps the seller where they were and adds no screen.

The missing-photos path stays as it is: add photos, submit, published.

Proposed, for the team to decide

The ad is never editable in the back office and in the funnel at the same time. See change 2.

Nothing changes in the back office. Advisors keep booking any ad as they do today, including one a seller is editing; the seller's side gives way instead.

1 - Edit the ad while it waits for review

Where: offers page draft card · waiting screen (funnel success) car card · the funnel Scenario: ad in review and not yet booked, or waiting for email verification

What changes

  • The in-review draft card on the offers page gets the same “Muokkaa tietoja” (Edit details) button the open and rejected draft cards already have.
  • The car card on the waiting screen (funnel success) after submitting gets the same button, next to “Avaa tiedot”. The card already has room for a primary button; today only the secondary one is used.
  • An ad waiting for the seller to verify their email is editable too, from the waiting screen (funnel success). It is otherwise ready and will publish as soon as the email is verified.
  • It opens the funnel at the equipment step, as the rejected-ad edit does, and the seller walks every step through to contact.
  • The last step saves instead of submitting: the ad stays in review, in the same queue position, and nothing is published.
  • The seller returns to the page they started from, the offers page or the waiting screen (funnel success), and sees production's success toast reading “Tarjouspyyntö päivitetty” (Listing updated). No new screen.
  • Once an advisor books the ad for its review call, the button is not shown. The card looks exactly as it does today. The seller is not told that the review has started.

One new string: “Tarjouspyyntö päivitetty”. The button label and the toast's design are production's.

See it in the prototype - and the booked state (booked=1 in any prototype URL, or Advisor booking on the prototype bar)

Acceptance criteria

  • The button appears on an in-review ad only while no advisor has booked it, and disappears once one has.
  • The button appears on an ad waiting for email verification, on the waiting screen (funnel success).
  • Saving leaves the ad in the state it was in, in review with its queue position, or waiting for verification, and does not re-run the review decision. The price step keeps the question the seller was asked when submitting.
  • The seller lands back where they pressed the button, with the save toast.
  • The ad is never editable in the back office and in the funnel at the same time; see change 2.

Why: A seller who spots a mistake or remembers a detail after submitting has no way to fix it until the review call, and an advisor may then review an ad the seller already knows is wrong.

2 - An advisor booking the ad ends the seller's edit

Where: the funnel, and saving the ad Scenario: an advisor books the ad while the seller is editing it Needs a decision

Proposed rule

  • No change in the back office. The ad stays in the advisors' review list while the seller edits it, and an advisor can book it at any time, as today.
  • A booking ends the edit. Once an advisor has booked the ad, the seller's next Jatka is refused. They land back on the view they started the edit from, the offers page or the waiting screen (funnel success), with a toast: “Muokkaus ei ole enää mahdollista. Soitamme sinulle pian!” (Editing is no longer possible. We'll call you soon!)
  • Steps saved before the booking stay saved. The advisor sees them when they open the ad.
  • From then on the edit button is not shown, as in change 1.
  • A page opened before the booking still shows the button. A seller can be looking at the offers page or the waiting screen (funnel success) with “Muokkaa tietoja” on it when an advisor books the ad. Pressing it then shows the same toast in place, and the button goes away, instead of opening the funnel.

The toast uses production's existing info-toast design. Its Finnish is a draft, pending approval.

Try it in the prototype - start the edit, then pick Advisor booking → Advisor books the ad on the prototype bar (the same control clears it again) and press Jatka.

For the team to decide

  • Whether the advisor sees that the seller edited the ad after submitting it, and ideally what changed. That would be the one change in the back office, and it is optional.
  • What happens to the step being saved when the booking lands. Production would refuse that save, so the seller loses what they typed on that step. The prototype keeps it.
  • The toast's wording.

What has to change behind the scenes

  • Saving the ad refuses a seller's save once the ad is booked, and returns a reason the funnel can turn into the toast. This is the only required change, and it is not in the back office.
  • Booking itself does not change. It takes all of one seller's in-review ads at once, so a booking can end an edit on a different ad from the one the advisor picked; the same refusal covers it.
  • A call logged without a decision puts the ad back in the list today. Whether editing reopens then needs a rule.
  • Fewer ad-quality rejections is the goal, so the reject-for-photos path may need a lighter alternative: asking the seller to add photos without rejecting the ad. Open.

Why: An advisor and a seller saving the same ad at the same time overwrite each other without warning. Letting the booking win keeps the advisors' work exactly as it is. Blocking the seller silently would read as a broken page; landing them back with a reason, and a call to expect, does not.

Notes for whoever builds this

  • The prototype's funnel step indicator is more generous than production's, and stays that way for now. In the prototype a seller can jump to any step they have started. In production the progress bar only goes back one step, and the equipment step, where editing a rejected ad starts, has no way back at all. Keep that in mind when judging how easy editing feels in the prototype.
  • The last step's button still reads “Lähetä ilmoitus” (Send listing) during an edit, although it saves rather than sends. It is production's label; whether it should change is a copy question for this initiative.
  • The rejection email's link could not be checked. It is sent through the marketing email tool, so its button target lives in Marketing's template rather than in the product code. Wherever it points, a seller landing on the funnel meets the same saved-position behaviour described above.

Open questions

  • Is the review decision re-evaluated after an edit, or are edited ads at least flagged, so the decision’s performance is not measured against data it never saw? Editing through the funnel makes this sharper: model year and fuel type can change, and both decide whether a car is reviewed.
    • Why it matters for the review algorithm. It scores the ad as submitted, and its results are measured on the ad as it went live: offers, accepted offers, the price, what the advisor found on the call. It reads almost every detail the funnel asks, so nearly any edit changes something it scored.
    • Which ads it reaches. An ad sent to review can be edited until an advisor books it. Advisors already change these ads in the call, so the data is noisy anyway; seller edits add a change nothing records. An ad cleared to publish without a call can be edited only while it waits for email verification, and that is the cleanest case of the problem: no advisor in between, and the decision being tested is that this ad needs no call. An ad from a verified seller publishes straight away, so it cannot be edited.
    • The bias has a direction. Sellers will add photos, fill in the service history or lower their price. An ad scored weak on ad quality or price can be fixed before its result is measured, so those scores look less predictive than they are. That matters most when the algorithm is tuned on this data.
    • Suggested for the first version: no re-run. Record that the seller saved the ad after the decision, with the time and ideally which details changed, so analytics can leave those ads out or report them separately. If a re-run is built later, it has to keep the original decision, because that is the one that routed the ad. How many ads this affects is unknown until the flag exists.
  • Should the registration number and mileage be editable? The seller enters them on the front page before the funnel starts, and the equipment step shows them read-only, so an edit cannot change them. Mileage also decides whether a car is reviewed. Today a wrong mileage can only be corrected by the advisor.
  • What happens when contact details change during an edit? The last step lets the seller change their phone number and, if they are not logged in, their email address. Advisors book by phone number: booking one ad books every in-review ad with the same number. For a logged-in seller, saving the last step also updates their account, not just this ad: the name, phone number and marketing choice they confirm there become the account’s, so a phone number changed in an edit changes it for all their ads. A save that carries an email address stores it, counts it as an email change and sends a new verification email to it, and the waiting screen (funnel success) offers its own change-email action only up to a set number of changes. Walking the funnel again should not send a second verification email or use up the seller’s changes.
  • Should editing during the auction be removed? Dealers bid on the car as described and are not told when it changes. Removing it would free the edit modal for editing before publish, which would also improve on the funnel in two ways:
    • The edit is linear. The seller changes what they came to change on one screen, instead of walking every step to the end.
    • The modal can leave out any detail that should not change after submitting. The funnel offers every field it asks.

Settled

  • Which details can be edited? Everything the funnel asks, because the edit is the funnel: equipment, location and delivery distance, tyres, keys, service history, repairs and damage, photos, price and contact details. That includes make, model, year, fuel type, drive and transmission, which the seller can correct on the equipment step. The registration number and mileage are the exception (above). Two rules follow:
    • The save passes the same checks as a publish: the photo minimum, and the asking price on an ad that will not be reviewed.
    • The review decision and the price estimate are not re-run, though the details they read can change (see the first open question).
  • Can the seller change their own price estimate? Yes, on the price step. An ad in review keeps the optional estimate, and an ad waiting for email verification keeps its required asking price, so an edit asks the same price question the ad was submitted with. No reason found to block it.
  • Should ads waiting for email verification appear on the offers page? No, and no seller could see them there. The offers page needs a login, an ad is only waiting while its seller has not verified their email, and the verification link logs the seller in and publishes the ad in one step. So the first offers page that seller sees already shows the ad live, and production’s draft list leaves these ads out anyway. The waiting screen (funnel success) is their only way to edit, which change 1 covers. The prototype works the same way.
