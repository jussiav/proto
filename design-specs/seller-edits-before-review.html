Design initiative · after the ad is submitted

Seller edits before review

Once a seller submits their ad and it waits for a review call, they cannot change anything about their car. This initiative lets them edit it while it waits, before an advisor picks it up.

Status: ideation · nothing specified yet · no prototype variant yet September 2026

How to read this

Nothing here is agreed yet. This page holds the direction so far and the questions still open. Changes will be added as numbered sections, each with a prototype variant, once they are shaped.

The prototype's default state has been matched to production for every edit flow first, so a future variant is compared against what sellers really get today.

Editing a rejected ad from the offers page takes two clicks today, and depends on timing. Both of its edit buttons first open the waiting screen showing the rejection; the seller then presses “Päivitä tarjouspyyntöä” to reach the funnel steps.

That happens because the funnel resumes the position it last saved, and that saved position expires two weeks after the ad entered review. After that, the same buttons land straight on the equipment step. The prototype reproduces the two-click path, since that is what production does. A new edit entry built on the same mechanism would inherit both behaviours.

Direction so far

Decided

Editing before the ad is published goes through the funnel. The auction edit modal saves to the published ad, and an ad waiting for review is not published yet, so the funnel is the editor that already owns this data.

Any link back to the funnel is enough to begin with. A link that opens a specific step would save clicks and avoid the timing issue above; it may join the initiative later.

Leaning towards

No new “ad updated” screen. After editing, the seller returns to the view they started from and is greeted with a notification that already exists. Production already shows a success toast when an auction edit is saved: “Tarjouspyyntö tallennettu” (Listing saved). Reusing it keeps the seller where they were and adds no screen.

The missing-photos path stays as it is: add photos, submit, published.

Needs more thinking

Lock the ad once an advisor books it for review. The advisor's review form saves the whole ad, so a seller edit landing during review would overwrite their work, or be overwritten by it, without warning.

And the reverse: while the seller is editing, the ad should not be bookable. The funnel saves step by step, so an advisor booking mid-edit would review a half-edited ad. The shape of this is open.

Notes for whoever builds this

  • The prototype's funnel step indicator is more generous than production's, and stays that way for now. In the prototype a seller can jump to any step they have started. In production the progress bar only goes back one step, and the equipment step, where editing a rejected ad starts, has no way back at all. Keep that in mind when judging how easy editing feels in the prototype.
  • The rejection email's link could not be checked. It is sent through the marketing email tool, so its button target lives in Marketing's template rather than in the product code. Wherever it points, a seller landing on the funnel meets the same saved-position behaviour described above.

Open questions

  • Which details can be edited? Equipment, service history, damage and photos look safe. Make, model, year and fuel type feed the review decision and the price estimate.
  • Is the review decision re-evaluated after an edit, or are edited ads at least flagged, so the decision's performance is not measured against data it never saw?
  • Can the seller change their own price estimate? It is the price question sellers most want to revisit.
  • Do ads waiting for email verification get the same editing? Today they do not appear on the offers page at all.
  • Should editing during the auction be removed? Dealers bid on the car as described and are not told when it changes. Removing it would also free the edit modal for editing before publish.
