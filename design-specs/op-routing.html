Design initiative · success page, decision page, emails

OP routing

A pilot: AutoVex suggests car insurance from OP at moments when the seller has nothing left to do with us. One block, one button out to OP's insurance page, and no change to the selling flow.

Status: designs accepted by OP · variant 3 in 3 placements November 2026

Part 1 · for the team

Summary

A seller selling their car will soon need insurance for the next one. We suggest OP's car insurance where the seller has nothing left to do with AutoVex, and send them to OP's own page for a quote.

One design, variant 3, in three placements. The same block goes on the success page after submitting, on the decision page after the seller accepted an offer or the offers were rejected, and in the emails that follow those two decisions. Variants 1 and 2 are retired; variant 3 keeps its name so earlier links and results still match.

How it is built. The two pages run as one VWO front-end test with two arms, control and variant 3 (part 2). The emails are sent from Klaviyo, so the block goes into Marketing's Klaviyo emails as an HTML block, tested with Klaviyo's own A/B split (B6).

It must not cost the selling flow anything. The block appears only where no action is asked of the seller, below AutoVex's own message on the page, and never replaces or delays a step.

The block

  • AutoVex speaks first. “Tutustu sillä aikaa suosittelemaamme tarjoukseen” (meanwhile, take a look at an offer we recommend) introduces the offer as ours.
  • The offer is the heading, with OP's round logo beside it: “Uudelle autollesi vakuutus −50 %”.
  • One button, filled with OP's orange, 56px tall: “Laske vakuutustarjous OP:ssa” (calculate an insurance quote at OP). “OP:ssa” says the click leaves AutoVex. It opens OP's car insurance page in a new tab; the AutoVex page stays open.
  • The terms line under the button: the offer covers the first insurance period of new comprehensive insurance.
  • A coupon frame: 2px dashed light grey border, 12px corners, white fill, 20px padding, at most 448px wide.
  • Shared with Huutokaupat routing: AutoVex's voice, the partner button as the only exit, never deterring the seller from what they still do with us. The frame and OP's mark off the button are this variant's deliberate departures.

Open questions

To discuss with the team

  • The email and page arms are not linked, and that affects the results. VWO and Klaviyo split sellers independently, so a seller can be in variant 3 on the pages and in control in the email, or the other way round. Neither test then has a clean control: a “control” seller may still have seen the block in the other channel. Options: link the two (the arm VWO stores on the seller's account passed to Klaviyo as a profile property, and the flow branched on it; needs the developers), or keep them separate and analyse sellers by what they were exposed to.
  • Which channel converts. Besides whether the block works, we want to know whether the site or the email brings the leads. That needs each placement's clicks, and OP's leads, counted per channel (B5).

With Marketing

  • Are the three Klaviyo emails live, and what do they say? The code only sends the events, so the block's place in each email is to be set against Marketing's template. Marketing may adapt the block's copy to its emails.

With OP

  • How are leads attributed to AutoVex, and what counts as a qualified lead? This decides what the button's link carries, including the per-placement parameters proposed in B5.
  • The target page. OP's car insurance page for now; a direct link to the calculator, or a landing page of OP's, may convert better.

With Legal

  • What, if anything, the link may carry to OP. Today it carries nothing about the seller. Any identifier or car data passed for attribution needs a legal basis and may need consent.
  • Does the block need a marketing label? It reads as AutoVex's own suggestion. Consumer law requires marketing to be recognisable as marketing and to show on whose behalf it is made; whether OP's name and logo are enough, or a label such as “Mainos” or “Yhteistyössä OP” is needed, is for Legal.
  • In the emails: the three emails are sent after a decision about a sale. Whether a partner offer in them makes them marketing emails, needing the seller's marketing consent, is for Legal.

Part 2 · build brief

Build the VWO test and the email block

Everything needed to build is in this part. The build code (B4) and the email markup (B6) are the source of truth: use them as written. The previews in part 1, the prototype and the email tool run exactly this code and markup.

B1 — What to build

  • One VWO front-end test, two arms. Control: the pages as they are. Variant 3: the build code as its JavaScript. Name the VWO variation “Variant 3” so results map to this page.
  • One campaign targeting both URLs (B2), with the same snippet, so a seller stays in the same arm from the success page to the decision page. The snippet finds which page it is on by itself.
  • No backend, Vue or stylesheet changes in the codebase. Nothing is stored and nothing about the seller is sent to OP.
  • Finnish only, like the copy. The snippet does nothing when the page's language is English.
  • The emails are separate: an HTML block Marketing adds to three Klaviyo emails, tested with Klaviyo's A/B split (B6). VWO does not reach email.

/* OP routing, variant 3: VWO front-end test snippet. Adds AutoVex's OP car

    return !!el && el.getClientRects().length > 0;
  }

  // Funnel success screen. The cream notice with the link to the offers page
  // ("Katso ilmoitukseni") is drawn only for a verified seller whose ad is in
  // review (Review.vue) or published (Success.vue). The unverified, rejected
  // and photos-missing screens have no such notice.
  function successAnchor() {
    var notices = document.querySelectorAll('div.border-orange-300.rounded-lg');
    for (var i = 0; i < notices.length; i++) {
      if (visible(notices[i]) && notices[i].querySelector('a[href*="offers"]')) return notices[i];
    }
    return null;
  }

  // Decision page. The hero's own title names the state; the snippet reads
  // nothing else about the auction.
  function decisionAnchor() {
    var titles = document.querySelectorAll('h2.text-3xl.font-bold.text-black');
    var hero = null;
    for (var i = 0; i < titles.length; i++) {
      if (DECISION_TITLES.indexOf(titles[i].textContent.trim()) !== -1 && visible(titles[i])) { hero = titles[i]; break; }
    }
    if (!hero) return null;
    var offers = hero.closest('section');
    if (!offers) return null;
    var next = offers.nextElementSibling;
    while (next && next.id !== ID && !visible(next)) next = next.nextElementSibling;
    if (next && next.id === ID) next = null;
    // The "Tarvitsetko apua?" banner, when the page puts it right after the
    // offers (accepted, or every offer rejected).
    if (next && next.tagName === 'SECTION' && next.querySelector('h5')) return next;
    return offers;
  }

  function injectCss() {
    if (document.getElementById(ID + '-style')) return;
    var style = document.createElement('style');
    style.id = ID + '-style';
    style.textContent = CSS;
    document.head.appendChild(style);
  }

  function place(anchor, tag, cls, inner) {
    var existing = document.getElementById(ID);
    if (existing && existing.previousElementSibling === anchor) return;
    if (existing) existing.remove();
    injectCss();
    anchor.insertAdjacentHTML('afterend', '<' + tag + ' id="' + ID + '"' + (cls ? ' class="' + cls + '"' : '') + '>' + inner + '</' + tag + '>');
  }

  function sync() {
    // Finnish only, like the copy.
    if (/^en/i.test(document.documentElement.lang || '')) return remove();
    var anchor = successAnchor();
    if (anchor) return place(anchor, 'div', 'av-op-success', CARD);
    anchor = decisionAnchor();
    if (anchor) return place(anchor, 'section', '', '<div class="container mx-auto">' + CARD + '</div>');
    remove();
  }

  function remove() {
    var existing = document.getElementById(ID);
    if (existing) existing.remove();
  }

  // Both pages are single-page apps that mount after load and re-render on
  // every action, so sync whenever the DOM changes. sync is idempotent.
  function start() {
    sync();
    new MutationObserver(sync).observe(document.body, { childList: true, subtree: true, attributes: true, attributeFilter: ['class'] });
  }
  if (document.body) start();
  else document.addEventListener('DOMContentLoaded', start);
}());

B5 — Tracking

  • Primary goal: clicks on the OP button, a VWO click goal on #av-op-v3 a.av-op-cta. Report it per page (success page, decision page) by URL, and the decision page per state if VWO can split by the hero title.
  • Denominator: sellers who reached a state where the block shows, in both arms. Control has no block, so count control's eligible views with a VWO custom goal or segment on the same hooks (B2), or compare clicks per visitor of the targeted states.
  • Guardrail, success page: nothing the seller still does is on that screen; watch that the published and in-review screens' own link to the offers page keeps its click rate.
  • Group membership is already recorded. When VWO applies a variation for a logged-in seller, the app stores the arm on the seller's account. The decision page needs a login, and a verified seller on the success page is logged in.
  • Leads at OP are counted by OP; how they are attributed to AutoVex is open (part 1).
  • Channel attribution, site vs email. Clicks are counted in two tools: VWO for the pages, Klaviyo for the emails. To see which channel actually converts, the click counts are not enough; OP's leads have to be split by where the click came from. Proposal, to agree with OP before the build: give each placement's link its own parameters, for example utm_source=autovex, utm_medium=web or email, utm_campaign=op-routing, and utm_content=success-page, decision-page, email-offer-accepted, email-offers-rejected or email-final-offer-rejected. They name the placement only, never the seller or the car. Until OP agrees, the build code and the email markup link to OP's page as it is.
  • Exposure across channels. The page and email arms are split independently (part 1), so report results per channel and, where the data allows, by what each seller was exposed to (page only, email only, both, neither).

B6 — The email block

  • Where: Klaviyo, in the flow emails triggered by the events OfferAccepted, OfferRejected and FinalOfferRejected. Marketing owns those flows. Add the markup below as one HTML block after the email's own message and button, before the sign-off and the footer.
  • A/B: in each of the three flow emails, a Klaviyo A/B test with two versions, control (no block) and variant 3 (the block). Name the version “Variant 3”. Track clicks on the OP link; Klaviyo's click tracking rewrites the link, which still lands on OP's page.
  • The logo is a hosted PNG, not SVG and not an attachment. Most mail clients do not show SVG (Gmail and Outlook drop it), and an embedded attachment shows up as a paperclip and is blocked or listed as a file by some clients. Upload op-logo-email.png (below; 160×160, transparent, shown at 40×40 so it stays sharp on high-density screens) to Klaviyo's image library and put its URL in place of OP_LOGO_URL. Klaviyo serves its library images over HTTPS from its own CDN, so nothing else needs hosting. The alt="OP" stands in when a client blocks images.
  • Built for email clients: nested tables and inline styles only; no flexbox, no classes, no web fonts required. The heading and button fall back to Arial where Barlow and DM Sans are not available (most clients). The arrow is the character “→”, since the page's arrow is an SVG. The button is a table cell with the link filling it, so the whole 56px button is clickable. The logo sits in its own fixed 40px cell with the gap on the heading cell, and the image carries min-width and max-width of 40px, so a client or template that shrinks images to fit their cell (max-width:100%) cannot squeeze it out of round.
  • Known differences by client: Outlook on Windows draws square corners and may draw the dashed border as a thinner line; the layout and colours stay. In dark mode, clients that invert colours may darken the white frame; the orange button and the logo keep their colours.
  • Width: up to 448px, centred, full width on a phone.

B7 — Do not

  • Build variants 1 or 2: they are retired.
  • Show the block anywhere else: not on the unverified, rejected or photos-missing success screens, not on the decision page while an offer can still be accepted, not for a B2B seller.
  • Pass anything about the seller or the car to OP in the link.
  • Change AutoVex's own content on these pages: the notice, the hero, the offer cards, the help banner, the auction result.
  • Rewrite the copy, colours or sizes in the build code, or the colours, sizes and layout of the email markup. In the emails, Marketing may adapt the copy to the email.
  • Put the logo in an email as SVG, as a data URI or as an attachment.

B8 — Acceptance criteria

  • Control: the pages are unchanged.
  • Success page, variant 3: every state in the B2 table matches its Shown/Hidden column. The block is 32px under the notice, 448px wide, above the Hotjar survey in review.
  • Decision page, variant 3: every state in the B2 table matches. The block appears under the help banner once the seller accepts or rejects, without a reload.
  • The button opens OP's car insurance page in a new tab, with no step in between; the AutoVex page stays open.
  • Re-renders never duplicate the block. No console errors. No horizontal scroll at 360px.
  • Emails: the variant 3 version of each of the three Klaviyo emails shows the block after the message, with the logo, in Gmail (web and app), Apple Mail and Outlook; the link reaches OP's page.
  • The goals in B5 record in VWO and the click counts in Klaviyo, per arm.

B9 — Check against the prototype

  • The prototype runs this exact build code: success page in review, published, decision page after accepting, after rejecting.
  • On production's own markup: success screen in review, published, unverified (nothing shown), decision accepted, decision rejected.
  • The emails: the email tool's offer accepted, offers rejected and final offer rejected.
  • The live result must match: same position, sizes, colours and copy.
