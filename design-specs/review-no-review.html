Review / No review

The funnel, the support page and the emails all tell the seller that our team reviews the ad and calls them. For sellers outside the review segment that call never comes — and most ads will soon publish without one. This page lists the changes that make our communication match the process the seller is actually in.

Status: live — 3 ready to build, 2 blocked on copy August 2026

1 — Price step: remove "Mitä tapahtuu seuraavaksi?"

The panel's whole content is a promise about a process the seller may not be in. Bullet 2 promises the review call to every seller — including the one being asked for an asking price precisely because their car is outside the review segment. Their ad publishes on submit and nobody calls, so the promise is broken by the next click. The panel comes out: the price step's job is the price question, and the process story belongs where the process is actually known.

What must be true afterwards

  • The heading and all five bullets no longer appear on the price step, in either price variant.
  • Nothing takes their place. No replacement panel, no new copy, no shortened list.
  • The same panel keeps working, unchanged, on the screens after submitting — in review, awaiting email verification, publish queue and success. Only this step loses it.
  • Decide the mobile case deliberately. On mobile this panel is the only reason the side column renders at this step, so removing it also removes the column and the step shows the price question alone. That is a consequence of the change, not a separate decision — confirm it is what you want before shipping.
  • Nothing else about the step changes: which price question the seller is asked, and where submitting takes them, are both untouched. No new stored fields, no new API surface, no new copy.

2 — Contact step: the submit button stops naming an outcome

The last click before submitting reads "Lähetä tarkastukseen", for every seller. It is already wrong for a seller outside the review segment, and once the seller-intent answer can downgrade a seller it is wrong for some inside it too. Replace it with one neutral copy.

Copy (FI approved · EN draft)

TodayLähetä tarkastukseen
Submit for review
ChangeLähetä ilmoitus
Submit listing

One copy, not three

An earlier draft varied the button by outcome — send for review / publish / submit. That was dropped deliberately: three copies would leak the decision one step before we are ready to state it, and the seller would learn their outcome from a button. One neutral copy promises nothing and denies nothing, which is why nothing on this step branches on the outcome at all.

What must be true afterwards

  • Every seller sees the same label. The segment and the intent answer are not consulted on this step.
  • When this step is opened from the offers flow the button is a plain "Valmis" — that case is untouched.

3.3 and 3.4 need a replacement, not a deletion

"Miten tiedän autoni arvon?" is the only place the copy answers what the car is worth. A seller who is never called still needs an answer, so the sentence stays and becomes conditional rather than disappearing. 3.4 repeats the same promise while discussing the asking price — it takes the same wording, so the two answers cannot drift apart.

Two decisions already taken

  • 3.5 keeps all four paragraphs. It answers a question only a seller who is in review would ask, and it is accurate for them. Only the framing moves from universal to conditional.
  • No new "why wasn't I called?" item. After 3.1–3.5 nothing promises a call any more, so there is no broken expectation to explain — and a new item would draw attention to a distinction the seller does not otherwise notice.

Nothing to change elsewhere. The front-page FAQ mentions no review and no call, and the offers/decision FAQ's only expert mention is general support rather than a scheduled call. Both were checked against the live CMS text.

5 — Lifecycle emails contradict the change, and nobody here owns them

A seller's inbox contains more than the codebase sends. Captures from a real seller's mailbox show a second family of emails going out through SendGrid, with copy that exists nowhere in the repository. At least two of them contradict what this initiative is changing everything else to say — including one that promises the call outright, in its subject line. They need an owner, a review and a fix.

Subject seen in a seller's inbox Why it matters
"Ilmoituksesi on nyt valmis tarkastettavaksi – soitamme sinulle pian!" Promises the review call outright, in the subject line, to sellers who may never be called. The strongest instance of the problem this initiative exists to fix, and it sits outside every surface the initiative can otherwise reach.
"Autamme sinua asettamaan autollesi pyyntihinnan" Promises help setting a price the team intends to remove from the product. Belongs to the asking-price initiative, but the same review applies.
"Luonnoksesi on tallessa. Viimeistele auton tiedot!" · "Melkein valmista, laita autosi myyntiin jo tänään!" · "Viedään tarjouspyyntösi maaliin!" Draft-abandonment nurture. Check that none of them describes the process in a way the funnel no longer matches.
"Näin otat laadukkaat kuvat autostasi" Photo guidance. No conflict found; listed for completeness.

What to look for when reviewing them

  1. Any promise of a phone call — "Soitamme sinulle", "asiantuntijamme ottaa yhteyttä", "puhelu kestää noin 5 minuuttia". Each needs the same treatment as the support FAQ: conditional wording, or nothing.
  2. Any claim that a listing is reviewed before publishing — "valmis tarkastettavaksi", "kun ilmoitus on tarkistettu". Most ads will publish without it.
  3. Any mention of an asking price or a price estimate. The asking price is being removed; a nurture email offering help with it will outlive the feature.
  4. Process descriptions with a fixed number of steps. A five-step list that includes the call is wrong for a seller who is never called, exactly as the support page's was.
  5. Timing claims — "36 tuntia", "1,5 työpäivää", "24 tuntia" — which must agree with what the product screens and the transactional emails say.
  6. Who owns the send. Three senders reach the same seller: the codebase's own transactional emails, SendGrid for lifecycle and marketing, and Klaviyo for events the code triggers. A copy change agreed with one owner does not reach the other two.
