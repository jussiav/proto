Review / No review

The funnel, the support page and our marketing emails all tell the seller that our team reviews the ad and calls them. For sellers outside the review segment that call never comes — and most ads will soon publish without one. This page lists the changes that make our communication match the process the seller is actually in.

A sibling initiative shares this step. Seller intent adds one question to the same price step, testing how ready the seller is to sell. It was built not to depend on Changes 1 and 2, and those have now shipped — so once the intent answer can influence who gets called, the price step is already making no promise about it.

Background — nothing to build against this

A review qualification engine now exists alongside the segment filters, and it is running in ghost mode: it scores drafts and records what it would have decided, while the existing filters make every real decision. Nothing in the seller-facing product reads it, and none of the changes on this page depend on it.

The intended next step is not a replacement. The filters keep priority; the engine adds fidelity within what the filters already allow. So the model on this page stays right: some sellers are called, some are not, and the communication has to work for both.

Why it is worth knowing anyway. The engine decides at publish, which is after the price step. The moment it starts influencing outcomes, the price step would have been making a promise about something not yet decided — which is precisely what Change 1 removed. With Changes 1 and 2 shipped, that transition needs no communication work at all.

The last click before submitting reads "Lähetä tarkastukseen", for every seller. It is already wrong for a seller outside the review segment, and once the seller-intent answer can downgrade a seller it is wrong for some inside it too. Replace it with one neutral copy.

One copy, not three

An earlier draft varied the button by outcome — send for review / publish / submit. That was dropped deliberately: three copies would leak the decision one step before we are ready to state it, and the seller would learn their outcome from a button. One neutral copy promises nothing and denies nothing, which is why nothing on this step branches on the outcome at all.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every seller sees the same label. The segment and the intent answer are not consulted on this step.
  • There is no second label to leave alone. This step passes one string, that string has one value, and it is the only submit label in the whole funnel — so the change is complete when that one string changes.

Asked and answered — is there a case where the button says something else?

No. Checked against the 7 September production snapshot: the step passes its label unconditionally, the key it reads has a single value, and no other step in the funnel passes a label of its own. The shared navigation component does have a fallback for when no label is given — “Jatka” — but this step always gives one, so it is unreachable here.

The near-miss worth knowing, because it is probably what prompted the question. This step does change for a seller who is already logged in with a complete profile — but only its heading and intro line, never the button. They read “Yhteystiedot” and “Tarkista yhteystietosi” (Contact details / Check your contact details) instead of the first-time pair. So that seller currently reads “check your details” above a button that says “send for review”. This change happens to fix that mismatch as well.

One other form could be mistaken for this one. The personal-details form on the offers side — the one that collects identity data for the EU tax directive — submits with “Vahvista”. Different form, different purpose, not part of this change.

5 — Marketing emails still promise the review call (for Marketing)

For Marketing · not a dev taskSendGrid · outside the productChanges 1–3 are live

In short

We are moving to selective review calling. Until now our team looked at every listing and called the seller before it went live. From now on only some sellers get that call, and most listings will publish without one.

That means our communication has to be accurate for the seller who receives it, instead of promising everyone the same thing. Changes 1–3 do this inside the product: the funnel and the support pages stop saying that a review and a call are coming. The marketing emails still say it — one of them in a subject line.

A seller cannot tell our senders apart. One email promising a call is enough to set the expectation for a seller who will never get one — which is the exact expectation this initiative exists to stop setting. What we need is one person on Marketing to own a pass over the lifecycle emails, using the two lists below.

Who does what. These emails are configured in SendGrid and only Marketing can edit them — they are not in the product's code, so no developer can change them and nothing here blocks Changes 1–3. Best done once those are live, so the emails are reviewed against what the product says by then. No wording is proposed on this page: that is Marketing's to write.

What to look for

Five things. Any email that does one of them needs changing.

Look for For example Why it is a problem
A promise that we will call “Soitamme sinulle”
“asiantuntijamme ottaa yhteyttä”		 Most sellers will not be called. They wait for a call that never comes, and then contact support about it.
A claim that we check the listing first “valmis tarkastettavaksi”
“kun ilmoitus on tarkistettu”		 Most listings will publish straight away. Saying a check comes first makes publishing look delayed or conditional when it is not.
A step-by-step process that includes the call A numbered list where step 2 is “soitamme sinulle” A five-step list is wrong for a seller who gets four steps. This is exactly what Change 3 fixed on the support pages.
Help with an asking price or a price estimate “Autamme sinua asettamaan pyyntihinnan” The asking price is being removed from the product. Separate initiative, same review.
Timing claims “36 tuntia”, “1,5 työpäivää”, “24 tuntia” These have to match what the product screens and our own transactional emails say. Easy to leave behind when the process changes.

How to fix a line

  1. First choice: take it out. If a sentence exists only to describe the review or the call, deleting it is cleaner than qualifying it. Nothing else in the email usually depends on it.
  2. Second choice: make it conditional. One word is enough, and this is what we did on the support pages: tarvittaessa (“if necessary”) or tapauskohtaisesti (“case by case”). Nothing is promised and nothing is denied, so the same sentence is true for both kinds of seller.
  3. Avoid a soft promise. “Saatamme soittaa” or “otamme mahdollisesti yhteyttä” still creates a wait. If we do need to reach a seller, we reach them — the email does not have to announce it in advance.

One thing to know before the pass. Three separate systems email the same seller: the product's own transactional emails, SendGrid for lifecycle and marketing, and Klaviyo for event-triggered emails. Agreeing a change in one of them does not change the other two, so the pass should say which system each email comes from.
