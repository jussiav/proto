Design initiative · decision page

Seller protection

A seller should be able to accept an offer without hesitating. Sellers who call AutoVex at this point already get help from customer service and sales advisors, but the page never says so. This initiative names that help “AutoVex-turva” and shows it on the decision page. Variants 1 and 3 go into a front-end A/B test; variant 2 is kept for later.

Status: for team review · copy is a draft · build brief ready November 2026

How to read this page

  • For the team: part 1 (summary, variant 1, variant 3, variant 2, open questions).
  • To build the test (a developer, or Claude Code pointed at this page): part 2, the build brief, is the complete instruction. Build only from part 2. Part 1 is context and decides nothing about the build.

Part 1 · for the team

Summary

What it is

  • A name for help that already exists. Nothing new is built behind it: “AutoVex-turva” is the help customer service and sales advisors already give sellers who call. Its phone number is in the “Tarvitsetko apua?” (need help?) banner further down the same page.
  • Variant 1 (section 2): a card under the offers, with an AutoVex-turva logo panel beside a short statement of support.
  • Variant 3 (section 3): the same card; its body names what the help covers.
  • In the production test: variants 1 and 3. Variant 2 (section 4), an “AutoVex-turvattu” row in every offer card that opens a modal, is kept for a later test.
  • Shown only while an offer can be accepted. Gone once the seller accepts or rejects.

Variant 1 in the prototype · variant 3 · variant 2 · production

Why

Take the hesitation out of accepting. Customer service and sales advisors name what makes sellers unsure at this point: whether to sell directly or trade the car in, how and when the car is handed over, why the offer is what it is, whether the dealership can be trusted, and less often a remaining car loan. After accepting, the usual question is timing: when the dealership calls, when it pays, what happens next.

Copy rules

The copy never names a particular risk or a problem the seller might meet with the dealership; variant 3 speaks of problems only in general (“mahdollisissa ongelmatilanteissa”). It speaks to certainty for accepting, not to what our process or the buyer makes necessary. A dealership renegotiating after acceptance does happen, but AutoVex is not a party to the deal; see the open question on what variant 3's body promises.

2 — Variant 1: AutoVex-turva card under the offers

What the seller sees

  • A card directly under the offer cards, above the auction result. The page's plain white container, with no outline: an outline is the offer cards' mark.
  • Two halves from 768px. Left, a light grey logo panel: the gold shield with the “A” of the AutoVex logo, “AutoVex-turva”, and a check with “Ilmaiseksi sinulle” (free for you), so the name does not raise “do I have to sign up or pay?”. Right, a headline and one short paragraph. Narrower, the panel sits above the text.
  • Same parts as the auction result block (48px mark, 20px headline, 16px body), different layout, so the two read as related and not as copies.

4 — Variant 2: AutoVex-turvattu row in each offer card (not in this test)

Not in the production test. Kept for a later test. Its build code is at the end of this section, outside the build brief.

What the seller sees

  • A row last in each offer card's list of facts, after the dealership's deal count and distance: the shield and “AutoVex-turvattu” (AutoVex-protected), underlined because it opens something. Every offer that can be accepted carries it, so it reads as part of what the seller gets with any offer here.
  • Pressing it opens a modal: the shield and “AutoVex-turva” as the title, one sentence on what the service is, five topics we help with, “Ota yhteyttä asiantuntijaamme” (contact our experts, which scrolls to the help banner), and last, under a thin rule, “Ilmaiseksi sinulle” to close the promise.

5 — Open questions

  • The test setup. The build brief assumes one test with three arms: control (the page as it is), variant 1 and variant 3. If the test runs variant 1 against variant 3 with no control, drop the control arm from B1; the snippets do not change.
  • What the help covers. Ops should confirm customer service and sales advisors can carry what each variant names, for every seller who asks: variant 1’s “aina auton luovutukseen asti” (right up to handing over the car); variant 3’s payment schedule, terms of the sale and “mahdollisissa ongelmatilanteissa” (any problems that come up), given that AutoVex is not a party to the deal; and, for a later test, variant 2’s five topics.
  • When the dealership calls. Timing is the most common question after accepting. A typical time (“usually within a day”) would answer it, but needs real data before it can be stated.
  • After accepting. Every variant disappears once the seller accepts, which is when the timing questions start. Should a version stay on the accepted page?
  • No contact link during a counter offer (variant 2). While a counter offer waits for the dealership, the help banner is hidden, so variant 2's modal has no way to reach us. Show the phone number in the modal then?
  • The name. Does “-turva” read as insurance or a guarantee? Legal and Marketing should confirm the name promises no more than customer service delivers.
  • The gold shield. Gold is not in the design system. Keep it as AutoVex-turva's own mark, or draw it in brand blue?

Part 2 · build brief

Build a VWO front-end test

Everything needed to build the test is in this part. The build code is the source of truth: use it as written. The previews in part 1 and the prototype run exactly this code.

B1 — What to build

  • One VWO front-end test, three arms. Control: the page as it is. Variant 1: the variant 1 snippet. Variant 3: the variant 3 snippet. Each snippet is the variant's whole JavaScript (its CSS, markup and behaviour) and stands alone. Name the VWO variations “Variant 1” and “Variant 3” so results map to this page.
  • Variants 1 and 3 differ only in the card’s body copy (B3). Position, layout, sizes and colours are the same; the two snippets differ only in the body and the element id (av-sp-v1, av-sp-v3).
  • There is no variant 2 in this test. Variant 2 in part 1 is kept for a later test.
  • No backend, Vue or stylesheet changes in the codebase. Nothing is stored.
  • One page: the consumer seller's decision page. Finnish only, like the page.
  • Audience: every visitor of that page. The snippets act only on consumer offer cards, so a B2B seller on the same URL sees nothing.

B2 — Where it goes

  • URL: https://autovex.fi/decision/<tender request id> (route user.offers.decision; logged-in sellers). Target /decision/*.
  • The page is a single-page app. The offers mount after load (behind a “Katso tulokset” screen on the first visit) and re-render after every action. The snippets watch the DOM and keep their elements in step; never insert twice.
  • Consumer offer cards are found by their distance row, the last fact row in the card's footer (“15 km (Helsinki)”): div.rounded-b-lg div.flex.text-xs.text-left.mb-4. The B2B card has no such row.
  • When to show: an offer is open for a decision while its card shows an accept button, a <button> whose text starts “Hyväksy” (“Hyväksy korkein tarjous”, “Hyväksy alhaisempi tarjous”, “Hyväksy lopullinen tarjous”). Accepted, rejected and expired cards show none.
  • Insertion point, variants 1 and 3: as the next sibling of the offer cards' own <section> (the one holding the cards), while at least one card can be accepted. The page grid puts 48px between sections.

The same rule, state by state. This table is a consequence of the rule above, not a second condition: build only the rule (an accept button on at least one card) and do not read the auction or offer status in the snippet. B6 — Tracking

  • Primary goal, all arms: offer accepted. The app already sends the VWO custom event offerAccepted when the seller confirms acceptance; use it.
  • Secondary goals, all arms: allOffersRejected (the seller rejects the auction) and sendNegotiation (a counter offer is sent). Both are already sent by the app.
  • Variants 1 and 3 send no events of their own.
  • Outside VWO: calls to customer service from sellers with offers open, before and during the test, to see whether naming the help changes them.

B7 — Do not

  • Build variant 2: it is not in this test.
  • Show the card on any other page, for a B2B seller, before the offers are revealed, or when no offer can be accepted.
  • Give the card an outline or border: an outline is the offer cards' mark on this page.
  • Change the offer cards' own content, the hero, the auction result or the help banner.
  • Add a phone number, email or any promise beyond the copy in B3.
  • Store anything.
  • Rewrite the build code's copy, colours or sizes.
