Design initiative · decision page

Seller protection

A seller should be able to accept an offer without hesitating. Sellers who call AutoVex at this point already get help from customer service and sales advisors, but the page never says so. This initiative names that help “AutoVex-turva” and shows it on the decision page.

Status: ideation · two variants · copy is a draft November 2026

Summary

Two variants

  • Variant 1 (section 1): a card under the offers with an “AutoVex-turva” ribbon, a shield and a short statement of support.
  • Variant 2 (section 2): a small “AutoVex-turva” badge directly under the hero's text. The hero already tells the seller to accept and that the dealership will call; the badge adds trust to that, and opens to what the help covers for a seller who wants to know.
  • A name for help that already exists. Nothing new is built behind it. The help is what customer service and sales advisors already give to sellers who call, and its phone number is in the “Tarvitsetko apua?” (need help?) banner further down the page.

Variant 1 · variant 2 · production

What it is meant to do

Take the hesitation out of accepting. Customer service and sales advisors name what makes sellers unsure at this point: whether to sell directly or trade the car in, how and when the car is handed over, why the offer is what it is, whether the dealership can be trusted, and less often a remaining car loan. After accepting, the usual question is timing: when the dealership calls, when it pays, what happens next.

Where it stands

Ideation. Variant 1 is the first version; variant 2 follows the team's feedback. A middle iteration (a seal on the page background with no container) led to variant 2 and is not kept as an arm.

  • A card directly under the offer cards, above the auction result, with no section heading of its own.

2 — Variant 2: AutoVex-turva badge under the hero

Ideation · draft copyDecision page heroOffers open for a decision

What changes

  • A badge directly under the hero's text, centred, above the offer cards. It adds trust to what the hero already says (“Hyväksy tarjous, niin autoliike ottaa sinuun yhteyttä”, accept the offer and the dealership will contact you) rather than repeating it.
  • Closed, it is a branded badge: the gold shield, “AutoVex-turva” in bold, the line “Tukenasi koko kaupan ajan” (with you throughout the sale) and a caret. White with a soft shadow and no outline: an outline is the offer cards' mark on this page.
  • Pressing it opens a panel under it: one sentence on who helps and when, five topics we help with, and a link “Ota yhteyttä asiantuntijaamme” (contact our experts). Pressing again closes it. It opens with the FAQ accordion's own transition.
  • The link scrolls to the “Tarvitsetko apua?” banner, which carries the phone number. The help is the same, so the number is not repeated. While that banner is hidden (a counter offer waiting for the dealership's reply), the link is left out.
  • Stays open when the page updates, for example after a negotiation action, until the seller closes it.
  • When it shows: while the seller has offers open for a decision: any auction result with offers, a final offer, a negotiation in progress or a closed negotiation. Not before the offers are revealed, after accepting or rejecting, or with no offers.

The topics are what sellers who call at this point ask about, as customer service and sales advisors describe it. Each is phrased as a subject we help with, never as a problem the seller might run into.

Acceptance criteria

  • The badge appears under the hero's text whenever the seller has offers open for a decision, and nowhere else.
  • It is a button that announces whether it is open; the panel is hidden from screen readers while closed.
  • The contact link scrolls to the help banner, and is not shown while that banner is hidden.
  • The open state survives the page updating after a negotiation action.
  • The badge has no outline; the shield is decorative.

Why: the hero is where the seller is told to accept, so the reassurance belongs beside it; the details are there for the seller who wants them, without making every seller read them.

What to read from it

  • Acceptance rate on the decision page, each variant against control.
  • Time to decide, from the offers being revealed to accepting.
  • Variant 2: how many open the badge, and whether those who do accept more or less often.
  • Calls to customer service at this point: whether naming the help changes how many sellers call, and about what.

Open questions

  • What the help covers. Ops should confirm customer service and sales advisors can carry each of the five topics for every seller who asks, including a remaining car loan and a trade-in.
  • When the dealership calls. Timing is the most common question after accepting. A typical time (“usually within a day”) would answer it, but needs real data before it can be stated.
  • After accepting. The badge disappears once the seller accepts, which is when the timing questions start. Should it stay on the accepted page?
  • The contact link while the help banner is hidden. During a counter offer the banner is hidden, so the panel has no way to reach us. Show the phone number in the panel then, or leave it?
  • The name. Does “-turva” read as insurance or a guarantee? Legal and Marketing should confirm the name promises no more than customer service delivers.
  • The gold shield. Gold is not in the design system. Keep it as AutoVex-turva's own mark, or draw the shield in brand blue?
