Design initiative · decision page

Asking price removal

The asking price is on its way out of the seller journey. The decision page still shows it twice above the fold — as a column of its own, and as a dashed line across the chart — using a row that duplicates, in different words and a different design, what the offers page already shows the same seller.

Ready to build — 2 changes, no new copy August 2026

How to read this

Two changes, both on the decision page’s auction insights block — the white card above the offers, holding a row of figures, a chart and the auction dates. Each section states what changes, links to that state in the prototype, and ends with a small-print Why.

No new copy. Change 1 adopts labels the offers page already ships; change 2 removes a line and a number. Nothing here needs a translation round.

The changes are independent, but doing 1 first is cheaper: it replaces the row that holds the asking price, so change 2 is then the only place it still appears in this block.

Some of this already exists behind a flag

Production already hides the expected-price column and the dashed line for one arm of a live A/B test. The variant does exactly what change 2 asks for, and half of what change 1 asks for.

So the likely path is: promote that variant — delete the flag and the branch rather than write anything new — and then do change 1 on top.

This initiative is wider than the test. The test removes a column. This asks for the row to be replaced by the one the offers page already uses, so the two pages stop describing the same two numbers with different words, different icons and different framing. That part is new work whatever happens to the test.

1 — The figures row becomes the one the offers page already uses

Decision page · insights blockAuction ended, offers seen

What changes

  • The three-column row above the chart is replaced by the two-column stats block the offers page shows during a live auction — same markup, same labels, same icons, same frame.
  • It stays in the decision page’s existing white card, in the same place above the chart. The chart and the auction dates below it are untouched.
  • The asking price leaves with the third column. No replacement figure.
  • The values are the same two numbers the row shows today — offers on this request, and how many dealerships they came from.

One thing to decide up front

The block is not a component today — it is markup inside the offers page’s auction timer card, which also draws the progress bar, the end date and the registration badge.

Extract the two-column block into a shared component and have both pages use it. Copying the markup across would work today and drift tomorrow, which is the thing this change exists to stop.

Acceptance criteria

  • The decision page and the offers page render the same component for these two figures — not two copies of the same markup.
  • The values are unchanged: offers on the request, and the number of dealerships behind them.
  • No asking price, expected price or price estimate appears anywhere in the insights block.
  • The chart, the auction dates and the surrounding white card are untouched.
  • It reads correctly at 360px — both labels fit their column without clipping.

Why: The same seller sees both pages within a day, and today they describe the same two numbers twice over, in different words, with different weight and different framing. Reusing the block settles that for free — and the column that has to go for the asking price to leave the page is one of the three it replaces, so the removal costs nothing extra.

2 — The chart drops the dashed asking-price line

Decision page · chartAuction ended, offers seen

What changes

  • The dashed horizontal line across the chart goes, along with the price label in the box at its left end.
  • Everything else stays: the rising bid line, the shaded fill beneath it, the highest-bid label at the right end, and the start and end dates below.
  • The chart’s vertical scale no longer needs to reach the asking price — with the line gone, the bid progression is what sets the top of the range.

See it in the prototype — the chart with only the bid line

Acceptance criteria

  • The chart draws one line, not two, in every auction state that renders it.
  • No price box appears at the left edge; the highest-bid box at the right edge is unchanged.
  • The bid line still fills the chart vertically when the highest bid is well below what the asking price used to set.

Why: The line invites the seller to read the auction as a race against their own asking price — a number the product is removing, and one that was never a prediction of what dealerships would pay. With it gone the chart shows what actually happened.
