Informed decision

A seller looking at an auction result has to choose between accepting, negotiating, rejecting and walking away — and the page gives them nothing to weigh that choice against. This is a first sketch of a section that does: not numbers, but the trade-offs between the routes a seller actually has.

Ideation only · not a build proposal · all copy AI-drafted September 2026

Read this before anything else

  • Every Finnish string on this page was written by Claude, not by AutoVex. None of it has been reviewed, approved or checked against the copy guidelines. It exists so the team has something concrete to react to instead of a description.
  • This is not a build proposal. There is no acceptance criteria section, no data contract and no dev hand-off. It is a switchable sketch, deliberately thin.
  • It deliberately contains no figures. No percentages, no euro gaps, no “x % of sellers”. The brief was to educate rather than to prove, and it also keeps the section clear of any statistic we cannot source.
  • Two of the six ideas make a promise about the dealership — a conditional sale while the seller finds a replacement car, and help with remaining finance. AutoVex does not control either. Wording is hedged to “agree with the dealership”, but OPS has to confirm both are realistic before this goes anywhere near a seller.

Try it

One new section, above the auction-details block. Switchable from the prototype bar under Informed decision (ideation), or by URL:

../decision.html?scenario=seen-offers&informed-decision=v1

Shown only where a decision is still open: the four price tiers, a final offer, and a negotiation the dealership closed. Not on no offers, expired offers, an accepted offer or a rejected one — there the argument is either useless or tactless.

Two blocks, because the ideas are two shapes

Splitting them was the only structural decision taken without asking. Four of the six ideas compare the seller’s routes to a sale, which wants a matrix. The other two are beliefs a seller treats as disqualifying when they are not, which wants a list. Forcing all six into one table made the last two read as claims about AutoVex rather than answers to the seller’s own objection.

Block 1 — the comparison

Rows are what a seller cares about; columns are the three things they can actually do with the car. A tick means yes, a cross means no, a dash means not always. Only the AutoVex column fills with green, which is what makes it read as advocacy rather than as a neutral table.

The pick-up row reads the seller’s actual offer. Pick-up is a property of the offer, so a seller who was not offered it is not told they get it: the row becomes “Paperityöt puolestasi” with a dash. Everything else is static. Two consequences worth a decision: without pick-up the row ties AutoVex with trade-in, so it may not be worth showing at all; and because an offer WITH pick-up is the only one shown on the page at all, the tick version of this row is currently unreachable in the prototype.

Block 2 — “these are not obstacles”

Three objections, quoted in the seller’s own voice and answered. All three are shown to everyone: framed as things sellers in this position weigh, a row that does not apply reads as information rather than as a wrong guess about them. Gating them on real data would mean asking questions we do not ask today.

”Minulla on jo parempi tarjous”

Onko kyseessä tarjous vai pyyntihinta? Myynti-ilmoituksen pyyntihinta ei ole summa, joka sinulle maksetaan.

EN: Is it an offer or an asking price? A listing’s asking price is not the sum you get paid.

”Tarvitsen tilalle toisen auton”

Voit hyväksyä tarjouksen ja sopia autoliikkeen kanssa ajankohdasta, joka sopii korvaavan auton hankintaan.

EN: You can accept and agree a timing with the dealership that suits finding a replacement. Needs OPS confirmation.

”Autossa on rahoitusta jäljellä”

Voit hyväksyä tarjouksen ja sopia autoliikkeen kanssa jäljellä olevan rahoituksen hoitamisesta.

EN: You can accept and agree with the dealership on handling the remaining finance. Needs OPS confirmation.

Choices made, and why

“Selling to a dealership is preferred by consumers” was reframed, not dropped. Stated as a research finding it needs a source, and citing research nobody has produced is fabricating evidence. The row now gives the reason consumers say that — no strangers, no test drives, no payment risk — which needs no citation. If real research exists, the original framing is stronger and should come back with the source attached.

Negatives are slate, not red. On this page red already means “something you have not seen” and amber means “your negotiation is live”. A red cross in a table would borrow a meaning that is taken. The cross and the dash differ by glyph alone.

It sits above the auction details, not below. The seller is being asked to decide; the evidence should arrive before the statistics about the auction, not after.

Open questions

  1. Does advocacy belong here at all? The section argues for accepting and sits directly above a reject button. docs/anti_patterns.md warns that pushy messaging costs trust, and docs/real_examples.md holds up a comparative line (“most sellers in your situation choose to…”) as the good pattern. The advocacy framing was chosen deliberately — but a neutral version is the obvious A/B partner.
  2. Is the fair-offer threshold the strongest row we are not using? It is not on the original list, but AutoVex already defines a fair offer internally and the seller is never told whether theirs clears it. That is a stronger, more specific signal than any row here.
  3. Second idea, not built yet: “similar cars sold for”. Recent comparable sales with the differences to this seller’s car spelled out. Deliberately parked until the comparison table is judged, and it needs real data rather than copy.
  4. On a phone the section is about 1 100 px tall. Nothing overflows, but it is a lot of reading above the auction details. Collapsing block 2 behind a summary line is the obvious trim.
