Seller intent

Whether a seller gets a review call is decided today from the car alone — fuel type, model year, mileage. Nothing in the funnel asks the seller how ready they actually are to sell. This initiative tests three ways of asking, one per arm, on the price step. Phase 1 only collects the answer: which answers should change the outcome is what the test is meant to tell us.

Why ask at all

What decides the call today

One boolean, can_review, computed from the car: TenderRequestReviewSettings (fuel types, model-year band, mileage band) → EvaluateDraftForReview. It is a whitelist — the car has to fall inside the band.

So two sellers with identical cars are treated identically, even when one is handing the keys over next week and the other is idly curious what the car is worth. The advisor discovers the difference on the call, which is the most expensive possible place to discover it.

Why the price step

  • It is the last step before contact details, so an answer here costs the seller nothing extra — they are already committing to submit.
  • It already asks about expectation, not about the car. The price estimate and "how soon could you hand it over" are the same kind of question; the equipment and photo steps are not.
  • It is where the review decision becomes unknowable. Once intent can influence the outcome, no screen at or before this step can honestly tell the seller what happens next — which is the subject of the separate Review/No review initiative.

Three arms, one question each

Every arm adds exactly one question below the price estimate, under a shared section header. v1 and v2 ask the same question in two formats — a five-option list against a date field — so the pair measures the input, not the wording. v3 asks a different question entirely.

Behaviour rules

  1. Phase 1 changes no outcome. The answer is recorded and nothing branches on it. Every seller who sees a question is still review-called, exactly as in control. Which answers should downgrade a seller is what the test exists to learn — guessing it now would bake the guess into the result.
  2. Optional, and visibly so. An info-icon line under the answers reads "Vastaaminen on vapaaehtoista eikä velvoita myymään." — the same treatment the estimate field above already uses for its own note, so both optional fields on the step say so the same way rather than one carrying a suffix on its label. Skipping is silent: no validation, no nudge, no blocked Continue.
  3. A skip is not an answer. A seller who skips is treated exactly as one who was never asked — for a review-segment car, that means the review call.
  4. Review segment only. A car outside the segment is not review-called either way, so it gets no question on any arm — it sees the asking-price step exactly as control renders it.
  5. Stored by id, not by label. Both the question and the answer are stable identifiers, so rewording an option never changes what was recorded and never invalidates collected data.
  6. The date arm has one control. A date or a skip — no options beside the field, so there is no second way to answer and no state where two answers disagree.
  7. Date bounds are today … today + 1 year. A handover date in the past is not meaningful, and a date beyond a year is indistinguishable from "later" for any routing purpose.
  8. Not part of the ad. The answer never appears on the ad preview sheet or the car card. It is information about the seller's situation, not content the buyer sees.
  9. The answer survives the walk. It is stored on selection, so going back to the price step re-selects it, and switching arm or leaving the segment clears it — an answer exists only where the question was actually asked.

Answer options, and what they will eventually mean

The Outcome column is deliberately unfilled. Phase 1 records answers and routes nobody; phase 2 fills this column from what the data shows. It is here as the shape of the decision, not as a proposal — a guess in this column would be read as a decision by everyone who opens this page.

Question Option id Finnish Outcome (phase 2)

Three outcomes exist in the model: review (the call), publish (autopublished, no call) and reject (not published at all). reject is declared and unreachable — the rejection path is parked until the screen a rejected seller lands on has been redesigned, which is its own initiative. For the date question, phase 2 needs thresholds rather than a per-option mapping; the prototype carries none, since it routes nobody.

Data contract

Aspect Rule
Two fieldsseller_intent_question and seller_intent_answer, stored separately on the draft. Keeping the question alongside the answer is what lets three arms write into one pair of fields and still be told apart later. The prototype uses sellerIntentQuestion / sellerIntentAnswer.
ValuesQuestion: handover-timing · handover-date · selling-situation. Answer: an option id from the table above, or an ISO date (YYYY-MM-DD) for handover-date, where dont-know is also a valid answer.
Both nullableAbsent means not asked or not answered, and the two are not distinguished on the draft — the arm assignment already records who was asked. Any consumer must handle null; most drafts will have it, since control is an arm too.
The outcome is server-derivedIn production the review decision stays where it is today — computed server-side when the draft is submitted. The answer is an input to that decision, never a decision itself, and the client must not be trusted to say what happens to an ad.
Prototype-only mechanicThe prototype derives the outcome client-side in one function, deriveOutcome({ inSegment, intentAnswer }), purely so the funnel is walkable end to end. It ignores intentAnswer today. That function is the single place phase 2 edits.
AnalyticsThe arm has to be recorded alongside the answer for the test to be readable at all — v1 and v2 produce different answer shapes for the same question, and control produces none.

What this initiative deliberately does not touch

This test ships first, so it must not depend on changes that land later. Two things on the very same screen belong to Review/No review and are left exactly as production has them.

The "Mitä tapahtuu seuraavaksi?" panel stays

It still promises the review call beside the question, on every arm. That promise is a real problem — it is Change 1 of the other initiative — but it is a problem that predates this test and applies equally to control, so removing it here would change what the arms are comparing.

The submit button copy stays

The contact step still reads "Lähetä tarkastukseen". In phase 1 that is still true for everyone who sees a question — they all get the call. It stops being true the moment intent affects the outcome, and the neutral wording is specced with the other initiative.

Open questions

  • How the winner is chosen — TBD. Three arms produce three different data shapes, so "which question won" needs a stated metric before the test starts, not after.
  • Phase 2's mapping and the date thresholds. Both come from the data. The date question in particular needs bands, not a per-option table.
  • Does the question become required? Optional keeps the funnel frictionless but leaves the most interesting sellers unclassified. Worth revisiting once the skip rate is known.
  • All Finnish copy in the table above is a draft awaiting approval, English included.
  • The rejection path is parked, not dropped. When it returns it needs a backend decision too: prod's rejected status today means "a human looked and it needs fixing" — one of its states is actionable and says Tietoja tarvitaan. A seller declined for low intent has nothing to fix, so reusing that status may collide with the offers listing, notifications and resubmission.
