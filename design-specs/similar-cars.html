Design initiative · offers page first, reusable elsewhere

Similar cars

Sellers wonder what price to expect. This block shows the highest offers that dealerships made on AutoVex for cars like theirs, so the expectation is anchored to real offers rather than to asking prices seen elsewhere. The table comes first; a row of photo cards is parked as a later alternative.

Status: direction decided — the table, and Variant 5 on the submit loader; data definition with Analytics, copy is draft Arms: control · v1 table · v2 photo cards (parked) · v3 accepted offers (parked) · v4–v6 submit loader, v5 chosen Prototype param: ?similar-cars= October 2026

In short

  • Decided (October 2026): the table, with each car's highest offer, comes first. On the offers page it lists the 3 newest similar cars per car, with no "Näytä aiemmat". On the decision page it shows 3 first and "Näytä aiemmat" opens more, up to 20. On the submit loader, Variant 5: one figure, the average highest offer of similar cars. The photo cards and the accepted offer are parked for later; their sections below stay as the record.
  • One block, two shapes, two figures. Variant 1 is a table and Variant 2 a sideways-scrolling row of photo cards, both showing each car's highest offer. Variant 3 is Variant 2's cards showing the offer the seller accepted instead. Variants 4–6 differ only on the submit loader: Variant 4 scatters a few cars around the "received" message, Variant 5 shows only the average of their highest offers, Variant 6 shows the cars in a carousel along the bottom. Everywhere else all three are the table.
  • Shown only while the seller is forming an expectation: ad in review, auction live, offers waiting for a decision or under negotiation. Hidden once the seller has accepted or rejected.
  • The figure is labelled with production's own words: "Korkein tarjous" for the auction's highest offer (Variants 1 and 2), "Hyväksytty tarjous" for the accepted offer (Variant 3).
  • Cleared by Legal, Marketing and Ops (October 2026): sellers' own car photos may be shown with the registration plate blurred, and the accepted offer may be shown.
  • One list per car. A seller with several cars sees a list for each car in review or at auction, each under its own make, model and model-year line. Unfinished drafts and decided cars get no list.
  • Three places: the offers page, the decision page and a submit loader after the contact step. On the decision page the block shows that car's list only, between "Tarjouskilpailun tulos" and the "Tarvitsetko apua?" banner with "Hylkää tarjouskilpailu", while the seller still has offers to decide on.
  • Latest first, in three recency bands: "Viimeisen 7 päivän aikana", "Viimeisen kuukauden aikana" and "Yli kuukausi sitten", counted in days back from today. Never "this week" or "this month", which would leave the first band empty early in a week or month. The band is a light cue for how recent the offers are, not a headline. Its label is quiet grey caption text with a calendar icon, and the load-more button says "Näytä aiemmat", so the order is legible before anyone reads a label.
  • Hidden below 3 cars. One or two cars read as a quote for the seller's own car, not as evidence.
  • What counts as "similar" is open and is Analytics' question. The block is built to work whether the answer is "same model only" or "comparable models".

Variant 1 — table

What it shows

  • Each list opens with the seller's car: make and model in bold, then the model-year range of the list, e.g. "Volkswagen Amarok · vuosimallit 2018–2020". No intro sentence — the section heading already says these are similar cars.
  • Make and model are not repeated in the rows when every car is the same model; the first column is then the model specification.
  • When models differ, the first column becomes "Auto" and each row leads with make and model in bold, the specification under it.
  • Columns: specification · model year · mileage · highest offer. Offer right-aligned and bold — it is the figure the block exists for.
  • Rows run latest first, stacked in recency bands. Each band opens with its label, left-aligned. The rows carry no rules of their own: a thin grey rule sits only above each band label, the first one being the column headings' own. A band with no cars is left out.
  • Band label: calendar icon + text, 12px medium, dark grey (slate-600), the same for every band. No fill, border, underline or blue, so it never reads as a link or a button and stays quieter than the cars.
  • On a phone (block narrower than 520px) the year and mileage columns fold into a grey line under the specification, leaving two columns. No sideways scrolling.
  • How many rows: on the offers page each car's 3 newest, with no "Näytä aiemmat". On the decision page 3 first; "Näytä aiemmat" adds the next 5, which are always the same age or older, up to 20 in all. The demo below is the decision page's.

Variant 2 — photo cards

What it shows

  • Each list opens with the same seller's-car line as Variant 1, with the arrow buttons beside it.
  • One card per car: photo, make and model, specification, year and mileage, and the highest offer at the bottom of the card, label left and amount right in large bold.
  • Make and model stay on one line (14px bold): names up to about 28 characters, such as "Land Rover Range Rover Sport", fit a 260px card; only a longer one ends in an ellipsis. The specification is 12px and at most two lines, so every card in a row keeps one height.
  • The price stays off the photo. These cards are much narrower than the ones dealers browse, so a price tag on the photo hid too much of it. The bottom of the card is the last thing read and stays level across the row.
  • The card is the design system's product card, unchanged — the same generic card the business seller's listings and the dealership's task list are built on, used through its own slots. The buyer-side and business-seller listing cards are wrappers around it with their own logic; this block does not use those wrappers, so it adds no case for them to support.
  • The photo is the front three-quarter shot — the first photo the funnel asks for, which the product treats as the main photo.
  • The registration plate is blurred before the photo is shown. In the prototype only the second card's photo has a visible plate.
  • The row scrolls sideways with the finger and snaps to a card. From 520px of block width, arrow buttons move it two cards per press.
  • Cards run latest first, side by side in recency bands. Every card is 16px from the next, across a band break too. Each band's label (the same as the table's) sits 6px above the band's first card. While a band's cards scroll past, its label stays at the left edge.
  • 5 cards first; the last tile is "Näytä aiemmat", which adds the next 5.
  • Prototype photos are placeholders and do not show the model named on the card. Real photos will replace them.

Variant 3 — photo cards, accepted offers

What changes from Variant 2

  • Each card shows the offer the seller accepted, labelled "Hyväksytty tarjous", instead of the auction's highest offer. So the list holds cars that sold through AutoVex.
  • The section heading is "Vastaavia autoja" (similar cars), since the cards no longer list offers in general.
  • The label sits above the amount. "Hyväksytty tarjous" beside the amount does not fit a 260px card, so on every card the label takes its own line and the amount stays as large as in Variant 2. The card is 20px taller than in Variant 2.
  • Everything else is Variant 2: card, photo, list headings, arrows, "Näytä aiemmat", and when the block appears.

What changes

  • The block repeats from the seller's-car line down: one list per car, in the order the page lists the cars (drafts in review first, then published cars).
  • One list per make and model. Two of the seller's cars of the same model share one list, under the first. No registration plate on the list: it belongs to the seller's own car, and next to "similar cars" it reads as if the list were about that exact car.
  • The rest of the page already repeats per car: one notification and one car card for each.
  • A car gets a list only while it is in review or at auction (live, offers waiting, negotiating). Unfinished drafts, accepted, rejected and sold cars get none.
  • Each list shows that car's 3 newest, with no "Näytä aiemmat" (the photo cards, parked, keep their own).
  • A car whose list is too short is simply left out; if no car has a list, the section is hidden.

In the funnel: the submit loader

A new screen between the contact step's "Lähetä ilmoitus" and the success page. It is production's loading screen (the one after the front page's registration and mileage form) with its own copy, and the submitted car's similar cars below it. One list, for that car.

  • While it "loads": the logo, the car animation, "Ilmoitusta vastaanotetaan" and "Muutama sekunti jäljellä". The similar cars are there from the start, below the message, so there is something to read while waiting.
  • After about 5 seconds: the heading becomes "Ilmoitus vastaanotettu" and "Jatka" replaces the "Muutama sekunti jäljellä" line in the same place. The space is reserved from the start, so the list below does not move when the button appears. Screen readers hear both states.
  • "Jatka" opens the success page in place of this screen, so the browser's back button from the success page goes to the contact step, never back to the loader.
  • Only when the ad was really submitted. If publishing is refused for missing photos, the seller goes straight to the success page's refusal. An edit saved from the offers page returns there as before. With fewer than 3 similar cars, the screen shows the message and the button only.
  • Variants 1–3 apply as they are; the logo is a plain image, as in production, so nothing on this screen leaves the flow before the success page.

Variant 4 — the message first, the cars as decoration (this screen only)

The headline, its change and the button are the screen; the similar cars only hint that other cars have had offers, without a list to stop and read. A seller who has read a list closely may press back from the success page to read it again, which in production leaves the funnel. On the offers page and the decision page Variant 4 is the table.

  • No car animation. From the top: a status icon in its own row, the heading, then the line or the button. The heading takes the success page's own heading style (Barlow bold, 24px), so the next screen continues it.
  • While loading: production's loading spinner (48px), "Ilmoitusta vastaanotetaan" and "Muutama sekunti jäljellä".
  • After 10 seconds: the spinner turns into a green check-circle (56px) that draws itself, the heading becomes "Ilmoitus vastaanotettu" and "Jatka" replaces "Muutama sekunti jäljellä" in the same place. Nothing else moves. The seller moves on with "Jatka"; the screen never moves on by itself.
  • The similar cars are a few grey cards without photos, scattered around the message and still. Each card: "Korkein tarjous", the amount, make and model, year and mileage, in muted grey, tilted a few degrees. Up to 8 on a desktop (left, right, above and below the message), 6 on a tablet and 5 on a phone (above and below it, the outer ones running off the edge of the screen). They never overlap the message or each other. They are decoration, hidden from screen readers.
  • Same rules as the other variants for when the screen appears; with fewer than 3 similar cars the cards are left out.

Variant 5 — the average offer, then on (this screen only) · chosen

Variant 4's screen without the cars: one small card tells the seller what similar cars have been offered on AutoVex on average. A teaser of data the seller will have access to, which may also make finishing worth it. On the offers page and the decision page Variant 5 is the table.

  • Same top as Variant 4: the spinner in its own row, "Ilmoitusta vastaanotetaan", "Muutama sekunti jäljellä".
  • Under it, a grey card: the chart icon and "Vastaavista autoista on tarjottu AutoVexissä keskimäärin", then one figure in bold, e.g. "25 900 €". The sentence says the figure is an average over other cars, so it does not read as an offer or a valuation for the seller's own car.
  • The figure is the average of the highest offers of the cars the decision page would list for this car (the newest 20 at most), rounded to the nearest 100 €. With fewer than 3 similar cars, the card is left out.
  • Nothing else on the card: no range, no time period and no note under it.
  • After 10 seconds: the green check, "Ilmoitus vastaanotettu" and the loading line goes. There is no "Jatka": 1.5 seconds later the success page opens by itself, in place of this screen, so back from the success page never returns here. The card does not move.

Variant 6 — the message in the middle, the cars along the bottom (this screen only)

Variant 5's screen with the cars themselves instead of the average, browsed in a carousel along the bottom of the view, so they stay secondary to the message and the button. Everything fits a phone screen without scrolling. On the offers page and the decision page Variant 6 is the table.

  • In the middle: the spinner, "Ilmoitusta vastaanotetaan", and directly under the heading the space for "Jatka". No body line and no "Muutama sekunti jäljellä".
  • After 5 seconds: the green check, "Ilmoitus vastaanotettu", and "Jatka" appears under it, in space kept for it from the start, so nothing moves. The seller moves on with "Jatka", which replaces this screen with the success page; the screen never moves on by itself.
  • Along the bottom: "Vastaavien autojen tarjouksia" as a section heading in the offers page's own style (bold, 20px, dark blue, with the chart icon), so the offers read as other cars' and not the seller's. Under it the cars side by side between two arrows: one on a phone, two on a tablet, three from 992px and four from 1200px wide. Each card shows when its auction ended (with the calendar icon), then make and model, model specification, year and mileage, and "Korkein tarjous" with the amount at the bottom.
  • Browsing: the arrows, or a sideways swipe, move the row by one card with a short slide (instant with reduced motion); they stop at either end. The count under the row reads "1 / 10" on a phone and "1–4 / 10" when several are shown. Below 360px wide the arrows go under the card, either side of the count. Latest first, the newest ten at most; the cards are a list, so a screen reader reads them all.
  • Fits without scrolling from 320 × 568 up, with tighter spacing on screens under 600px tall. With fewer than 3 similar cars the carousel is left out.

Built from what exists

Nothing in the product shows a consumer seller other cars' offers, so the block itself is new. Every part inside it is an existing pattern:

  • Table frame — the offers page's own white auction card; row dividers from the one existing consumer-side table.
  • Photo card — the product card the business seller's listings already use, with its 16:9 photo.
  • Highest offer — in the product card's own footer slot, the bottom of the white area. Amount enlarged.
  • "Näytä aiemmat" and the arrows — the standard secondary button.
  • Recency band label — caption text with production's calendar icon.
  • Labels "Mallitarkennus", "Vuosimalli", "Ajokilometrit" and "Korkein tarjous" are production copy. "Näytä aiemmat" replaces production's "Näytä lisää" and is a draft.

Data the block needs

One list per eligible car of the seller, already filtered. The block decides nothing about similarity; it orders each list latest first and splits it into the recency bands.

Open questions

For Analytics

  • How similar? Same model and specification, same model only, or comparable models — and which year and mileage band around the seller's car.
  • How many cars does each definition give for a typical seller, and in what time window? This sets whether 3 is the right minimum.
  • Settled — sort order: latest first, in three recency bands. Sorting by price would anchor to the highest; sorting by closeness needs a distance measure.
  • Spread check. If the list ranges widely, the block may anchor to the top rather than inform. Is there a band outside which a car should be left out?

For the team

  • Settled — how many: 3 per car on the offers page with no "Näytä aiemmat"; 3 first and up to 20 on the decision page. Open: how many each "Näytä aiemmat" adds; the prototype uses 5.
  • Variant 5: average or median, and which offers? "Keskimäärin" describes an average (mean); a median, which one very high offer moves less, would need other words. The figure is the highest offers only, while the sentence says "tarjottu", which a seller may read as all offers. Over how many cars and how far back is for Analytics.
  • Variant 5 moves on by itself. A normal loader pattern once the work is "done", but a seller reading the card is moved mid-sentence; 10 seconds is meant to be enough.
  • Settled — Variant 4 and moving content: the cards are still. The only motion is the spinner, which runs while the screen offers nothing to do, the case WCAG 2.2.2 counts as essential, and stops when "Jatka" appears.
  • Submit loader: how long, and where does "Jatka" sit? 5 seconds and in place of the loading line, above the list, so it is visible without scrolling. Below the list would make more sellers see the cars but hide the way on. The wait also delays the success page's email verification for sellers who still need it.
  • More details? Fuel, transmission or drive could help a seller judge similarity; each one costs a column on a phone.
  • Show the seller's own car as a reference row at the top, without a price?
  • Settled — other sellers' photos and offers: Legal, Marketing and Ops cleared showing sellers' own car photos with the registration plate blurred, and showing the accepted offer (October 2026).
  • Settled for now — highest offer. The accepted offer (Variant 3) is parked for later.
