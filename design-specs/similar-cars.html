Design initiative · offers page first, reusable elsewhere

Similar cars

Sellers wonder what price to expect. This block shows the highest offers that dealerships made on AutoVex for cars like theirs, so the expectation is anchored to real offers rather than to asking prices seen elsewhere. Two shapes to compare: a table and a row of photo cards.

Status: ideation — data definition with Analytics, copy is draft Arms: control · v1 table · v2 photo cards Prototype param: ?similar-cars= October 2026

In short

  • One block, two shapes. Variant 1 is a table, Variant 2 a sideways-scrolling row of photo cards. Both show the same data and the same caveat.
  • Shown only while the seller is forming an expectation: ad in review, auction live, offers waiting for a decision or under negotiation. Hidden once the seller has accepted or rejected.
  • The figure is always the auction's highest offer, labelled with the product's existing "Korkein tarjous" — never a sale price.
  • One list per car. A seller with several cars sees a list for each car in review or at auction, each under its own make, model and model-year line. Unfinished drafts and decided cars get no list.
  • Hidden below 3 cars. One or two cars read as a quote for the seller's own car, not as evidence.
  • What counts as "similar" is open and is Analytics' question. The block is built to work whether the answer is "same model only" or "comparable models".

Variant 1 — table

What it shows

  • Each list opens with the seller's car: its registration plate badge (the same badge as on the seller's own car card), make and model in bold, then the model-year range of the list, e.g. "Volkswagen Amarok · vuosimallit 2018–2020". No intro sentence — the section heading already says these are similar cars.
  • Make and model are not repeated in the rows when every car is the same model; the first column is then the model specification.
  • When models differ, the first column becomes "Auto" and each row leads with make and model in bold, the specification under it.
  • Columns: specification · model year · mileage · highest offer. Offer right-aligned and bold — it is the figure the block exists for.
  • On a phone (block narrower than 520px) the year and mileage columns fold into a grey line under the specification, leaving two columns. No sideways scrolling.
  • 5 rows first; "Näytä lisää" adds 5 at a time.

Variant 2 — photo cards

What it shows

  • Each list opens with the same seller's-car line as Variant 1, with the arrow buttons beside it.
  • One card per car: photo, a "Korkein tarjous" row under it, make and model, specification, year and mileage.
  • The photo is the front three-quarter shot — the first photo the funnel asks for, which the product treats as the main photo.
  • The registration plate is blurred before the photo is shown. In the prototype only the second card's photo has a visible plate.
  • The row scrolls sideways with the finger and snaps to a card. From 520px of block width, arrow buttons page it.
  • 5 cards first; the last tile is "Näytä lisää", which adds 5.
  • Prototype photos are placeholders and do not show the model named on the card. Real photos will replace them.

A seller with several cars

What changes

  • The block repeats from the seller's-car line down: one list per car, in the order the page lists the cars (drafts in review first, then published cars). The plate badge tells two cars of the same model apart.
  • The rest of the page already repeats per car: one notification and one car card for each.
  • A car gets a list only while it is in review or at auction (live, offers waiting, negotiating). Unfinished drafts, accepted, rejected and sold cars get none.
  • Each list has its own "Näytä lisää". The caveat line appears once, at the end.
  • A car whose list is too short is simply left out; if no car has a list, the section is hidden.

When it appears on the offers page

Its own section, "Vastaavien autojen tarjouksia", directly under the seller's own cars and above the FAQ. The rules apply per car.

Seller's stateBlockWhy
Ad in reviewShownEarliest moment the seller wonders what to expect
Auction liveShownExpectation forms while waiting for the result
Offers waiting for a decision, negotiating, final offerShownThe seller is judging their own offer
Accepted, deal completed, all rejectedHiddenThe decision is made; other cars' offers can only cause regret
Unfinished draft, rejected draft, no adHiddenThe car's details are not final, so similar cars cannot be picked reliably
Fewer than 3 similar carsHiddenToo thin to be evidence

Built from what exists

Nothing in the product shows a consumer seller other cars' offers, so the block itself is new. Every part inside it is an existing pattern:

  • Table frame — the offers page's own white auction card; row dividers from the one existing consumer-side table.
  • Photo card — the product card the business seller's listings already use, with its 16:9 photo.
  • "Korkein tarjous" row — the row that already shows a business seller the highest offer on their own listing card. One deliberate change: it sits under the photo instead of over its bottom edge, where on a 260px card it covered a third of the photo.
  • Caveat line — the funnel's blue info-icon line.
  • "Näytä lisää" and the arrows — the standard secondary button.
  • Labels "Mallitarkennus", "Vuosimalli", "Ajokilometrit", "Korkein tarjous" and "Näytä lisää" are production copy.

Data the block needs

One list per eligible car of the seller, already filtered and sorted. The block decides nothing about similarity.

FieldNotes
make, modelIf all rows share them, the table states them once
model_specificationRequired — the main way a seller tells cars apart
year, drivenModel year, km
highest_offerThe auction's highest offer in euros. Not the accepted price, not a sale price
imageVariant 2 only. The front three-quarter photo (the funnel's first photo), plate blurred server-side before it reaches the browser
auction end dateNot shown today; needed for sorting and a time window

Open questions

For Analytics

  • How similar? Same model and specification, same model only, or comparable models — and which year and mileage band around the seller's car.
  • How many cars does each definition give for a typical seller, and in what time window? This sets whether 3 is the right minimum.
  • Sort order. The prototype sorts by most recent auction. Sorting by price would anchor to the highest; sorting by closeness needs a distance measure.
  • Spread check. If the list ranges widely, the block may anchor to the top rather than inform. Is there a band outside which a car should be left out?

For the team

  • How many first, how many per "Näytä lisää"? The prototype uses 5 and 5.
  • More details? Fuel, transmission or drive could help a seller judge similarity; each one costs a column on a phone.
  • Show the seller's own car as a reference row at the top, without a price?
  • May we show other sellers' photos at all, even with the plate blurred? Variant 2 depends on the answer. Photos can also show a home, a street or a person, and offers on another seller's car are that seller's information. This needs a privacy and legal review before anything ships; this spec takes no position on it.
